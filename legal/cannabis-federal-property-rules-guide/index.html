Federal-property cannabis jurisdiction guide

State permission stops.
Federal property starts.

A state map cannot answer a federal-site question. Identify the property owner, managing agency, exact product and current federal rule before carrying anything across the boundary.

Property-specific orders, conditions of entry, security rules, employment rules and state law may add restrictions. Check the managing agency and current official text before acting.

THE USEFUL PART

Keep these in your head.

  • A place inside a cannabis-legal state may still be federal property. State adult-use permission does not automatically control a national park, federal building, courthouse, military installation or federal workplace.
  • Identify the managing agency and boundary before the product crosses it. A visitor center, concession, road, parking lot and neighboring state land may not share the same rule.
  • The April 28, 2026 federal rule moved defined FDA-approved marijuana products and marijuana subject to qualifying state medical licenses to Schedule III; it did not create blanket adult-use permission on federal property.
  • National Park Service regulation 36 CFR 2.35 separately addresses delivery, possession and dangerous intoxication in park areas. Read the current text and park-specific notices rather than relying on a slogan.
  • Do not mail cannabis, cross a border, board a flight or enter another jurisdiction based on a federal-property answer. Each is a separate legal route.

Read the 2026 scheduling change narrowly

On April 28, 2026, a federal final rule placed FDA-approved drug products containing marijuana and marijuana subject to a qualifying state medical-marijuana license in Schedule III. The rule also established federal registration requirements for state medical licensees and maintained permit controls for import and export.

The same final rule states that marijuana outside those defined lanes remains Schedule I. A separate proceeding concerning broader rescheduling continued in 2026. The practical conclusion is modest: do not use an old “all marijuana is Schedule I” sentence, but do not turn a limited medical rescheduling rule into general permission to possess, use, share, sell or transport cannabis on federal property.

Scheduling is not a property pass

CSA schedule, lawful acquisition, agency regulation, a park-specific order, employment policy and conditions of entry are different layers. An answer in one layer does not erase the others.

National parks use their own regulation

36 CFR 2.35 prohibits delivery of a controlled substance in a park area except lawful practitioner distribution. It also prohibits possession unless the substance was obtained directly or by a valid prescription or order from a practitioner acting professionally, or possession is otherwise allowed by federal or state law. Being under the influence to a degree that may endanger a person, property or park resources is prohibited.

That text should be read with the current Controlled Substances Act status, the park's governing state law, park compendium and any closures or special regulations. A state medical card, retail receipt or hemp label is not a substitute for checking whether the exact possession is allowed. Never drive impaired, use where smoking or vaping is prohibited, distribute to another person or assume a campground erases the rule.

Federal buildings add conduct and entry rules

Federal-property conduct rules can address narcotic drugs, alcohol, disorderly conduct and compliance with posted signs and lawful directions. Buildings may also impose screening rules that are stricter than what can be carried on an ordinary public sidewalk. Courthouses, secure facilities and military installations publish their own visitor conditions.

  1. Find the agency.

    Use the building or site's official page, not a travel blog or map review.

  2. Find the visitor rule.

    Check prohibited items, screening, storage, smoking, medicine and accessibility instructions.

  3. Name the product.

    State adult-use marijuana, state medical marijuana, an FDA-approved prescription drug and a hemp-derived product can occupy different legal categories.

  4. Keep proof in its proper lane.

    A prescription label may matter for a medicine; a retail package or certificate is not a prescription or federal permission.

  5. Ask before arrival.

    If a lawful medicine or disability accommodation is involved, use the official contact and do not disclose more health information than necessary to an unverified person.

Do not use “hemp” as a universal exception

Hemp has a federal statutory definition, but a retail word on the package does not prove the product meets it. State rules can be narrower, and federal food, drug and aviation rules still apply. The 2026 federal final rule also notes that the statutory hemp definition changes on November 12, 2026, so a page or package written earlier may be stale.

  • Identify the exact cannabinoid source and final product—not only “hemp extract.”
  • Keep delta-9 THC concentration basis, total package amount and current statutory definition separate.
  • Match the lot report and recognize that laboratory scope and uncertainty still matter.
  • Check the state and property rule in effect on the date of entry.
  • Do not carry a hemp/CBD product into a federal workplace or safety-sensitive role without checking policy and drug-testing implications.
