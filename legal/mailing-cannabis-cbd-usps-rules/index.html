Federal mailing explainer

Can you mail it?
Start with what it is.

State legality does not answer a federal mailing question. USPS treats controlled substances and qualifying hemp under different rules, and the sender carries the compliance burden.

11 min read Reviewed September 14, 2026Adults 21+

ON THIS PAGE

Reviewed September 14, 2026. This is general education, not legal advice or permission to ship a product. Check the current USPS Publication 52, federal law, origin and destination law, and qualified counsel when consequences matter.

THE USEFUL PART

Keep these in your head.

  • Do not put marijuana in ordinary consumer mail merely because adult use or medical use is legal in one or both states.
  • USPS permits qualifying hemp and hemp-derived products in domestic mail only when the mailer complies with all applicable law and keeps specified records for at least three years.
  • USPS prohibits hemp and CBD in international mail, including overseas APO, FPO, and DPO addresses.
  • A product name, QR code, old lab report, sealed box, or retailer promise does not establish mailability; the exact item and route matter.

The September 2026 USPS baseline

Reviewed September 14, 2026. USPS Publication 52 section 453 is the live operational source checked for this page.

Publication 52 says that when distribution of a controlled substance is unlawful under the federal Controlled Substances Act or its implementing regulations, mailing it is also unlawful. It limits domestic mailing of controlled substances to narrow authorized situations such as qualified DEA registrants, exempt official duties, authorized mail-back programs, and certain properly mailed prescription medicines.

That is not a consumer exception for marijuana bought lawfully under state adult-use or medical law. State permission and a federal mailing authorization are different questions. Do not infer that a sealed package, small amount, personal gift, medical card, or legal destination makes an ordinary marijuana mailing lawful.

Domestic hemp has conditions, not a blanket green light

Publication 52 section 453.37 allows hemp and hemp-based products, including CBD within the federal THC limit referenced there, in domestic mail only when the mailer complies with all applicable federal, state, and local law, including USDA-approved production plans, and retains records establishing compliance for no less than three years after mailing.

USPS names examples such as laboratory results, licenses, or compliance reports. The rule does not say that any random PDF is enough. A useful record package ties the exact product and lot to the producer, lawful production or handling chain, relevant test, dates, method, units, basis, signatures or status, and current origin and destination rules.

  • Match the product name, producer, lot, and format across package and report.
  • Read delta-9 THC, THCA or other required analytes, calculation basis, dry-weight basis, uncertainty, qualifiers, and reporting limits under the rule that actually applies.
  • Confirm the laboratory and report verification route rather than trusting a screenshot or editable file.
  • Check that state or local law does not restrict the product, formulation, age, sale, possession, or delivery at either end.
  • Keep the required records for the full USPS period; do not discard them when tracking shows delivery.

The THCA versus THC guide explains why analytes and legal formulas cannot be collapsed into one marketing number.

International means no for USPS hemp and CBD

Publication 52 states that shipments of hemp and hemp-based products, including CBD, are prohibited in international mail. It expressly includes items addressed to overseas Army Post Office, Fleet Post Office, and Diplomatic Post Office locations.

Do not treat an APO, FPO, or DPO address as a domestic hemp loophole. Do not rely on a destination country's retail market, traveler allowance, product availability, or a seller willing to print a label. Customs declarations must be accurate, and an international prohibition is not solved by omitting, disguising, or relabeling the contents.

If a person already has an international shipment in motion, use official USPS and customs channels and obtain legal advice rather than attempting to reroute or misdescribe it.

Why two legal states do not create a legal mailing lane

A package enters a federal postal system. The laws of the origin and destination do not erase federal controlled-substance and mailability rules. A state cannabis license authorizes only the activities and geography the issuing program covers; it is not a general federal shipping license.

  • “IT IS A GIFT”No payment does not transform a controlled substance into ordinary mailable matter.
  • “IT IS MEDICAL”A patient credential is not the same as an authorized prescription-drug mailer or federal registration.
  • “BOTH STATES ARE LEGAL”Interstate movement and federal mailing remain separate questions.
  • “THE PACKAGE IS ODOR-PROOF”Concealment, packaging, or lack of odor does not create legal status.
  • “THE LABEL SAYS HEMP”The exact product and supporting compliance record—not one word—must satisfy the governing rules.

For state-level possession and program starting points, use the dated 50-state law library, then open the current official source for both locations.

Prescription and mail-back routes are narrow

Publication 52 permits certain prescription medicines containing controlled substances when mailed by drug manufacturers or registered agents, pharmacies, medical practitioners, or other authorized dispensers under applicable law. It also describes separately authorized mail-back programs operated by qualified collectors.

A patient should not copy those rules into a homemade return or forward a prescription product to another person. Ask the dispensing pharmacy or manufacturer for the exact, current return or delivery process. Use only supplied or formally approved mail-back packaging where required. Never put loose cannabis, a vape cartridge, an edible, or an unknown product into a medication-return envelope.

FDA approval of one cannabinoid medicine does not make every CBD oil, cannabis product, or similarly named item an approved prescription drug. Product identity and authorized parties matter.

Packaging comes after eligibility

Secure packaging is required for items that are actually mailable, but packaging cannot cure a prohibited shipment. If a lawful hemp mailing qualifies, follow current USPS general packaging standards and any product-specific requirements. Prevent leakage, dislodgement, breakage, contamination, and exposure; keep documentation organized and declarations truthful.

Lithium batteries, alcohol-based liquids, aerosols, solvents, and other hazardous materials may trigger separate rules. Do not assume that classifying plant material also resolves a vape, battery, tincture, topical, or mixed-product kit. Check every component.

A cannabis storage jar or lockbox is not a shipping container by default. The stash-jar guide covers household storage and makes no mailability claim.

A stop-or-verify checklist

  1. Name the exact item.

    Finished product, ingredients, cannabinoid form, concentration, lot, producer, and intended route.

  2. Classify it under current federal law.

    Do not let state labels or retail categories answer this step.

  3. Open current Publication 52.

    Read section 453 and every cross-reference that applies on the day of mailing.

  4. Check origin, destination, and local law.

    Sale, possession, delivery, age, formulation, and licensing rules can differ.

  5. Verify records.

    Match the exact lot to the complete laboratory, licensing, and compliance package and preserve it for the required period.

  6. Check every component.

    Batteries, liquids, aerosols, alcohol, solvents, glass, and temperature-sensitive contents may have other restrictions.

  7. Stop when uncertain.

    Ask USPS Product Classification, the relevant regulators, and qualified counsel before tendering the package.

This checklist is designed to prevent an unlawful mailing, not to help anyone avoid inspection or enforcement.
