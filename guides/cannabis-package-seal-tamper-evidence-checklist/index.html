Cannabis package guide

Inspect the package.
Preserve the evidence.

Tamper evidence can show that a package was opened or altered; it cannot prove the contents are safe. Use one pre-open record for the seal, closure, label, lot, condition, seller, report, and exit route.

General package inspection and recordkeeping—not a contamination test, legal conclusion, return guarantee, or instruction to use a questionable product. Do not taste, inhale, energize, heat, open, or transfer a product to investigate damage. Emergency, poison, fire, battery, and medical response outrank documentation.

THE USEFUL PART

Keep these in your head.

  • Tamper-evident, child-resistant, resealable, sealed, and intact describe different jobs; one feature does not prove the others.
  • Inspect and photograph the unopened package, closure, seal, label, lot, dates, product condition, receipt, and seller before breaking the evidence.
  • A normal-looking seal cannot verify identity, potency, contaminant status, freshness, storage history, or safety.
  • If something is wrong, stop, isolate safely, preserve the record, follow the licensed seller or regulator route, and do not mail or cross state lines with cannabis.

Use the stop gate before the checklist

  • MOVE AWAY AND CALL EMERGENCY SERVICESActive fire, smoke, hissing, venting, severe battery heat, dangerous chemical release, severe trouble breathing, collapse, seizure, or another immediate threat.
  • CALL POISON CONTROLIn the United States, 1-800-222-1222 for an unexpected exposure or product question; call 911 for emergency signs.
  • DO NOT OPEN OR USEBroken or missing seal, leak, bulge, puncture, unexplained residue, foreign material, severe odor, broken glass, damaged battery device, wrong product, recall match, or package identity that does not reconcile.

Keep children, pets, heat, flame, water, food-preparation surfaces, vehicles, and uninvolved people away. Do not wipe away the only evidence, recharge a damaged device, sniff closely, or open a package “just to check.”

Separate five packaging jobs

FeatureIts jobIt cannot prove by itself
Tamper-evidentProvides a visible indicator or barrier whose breach can show opening or alterationWho opened it, when, why, or whether contents are safe
Child-resistantMeets an applicable special-packaging standard and instructionsChildproofing, tamper evidence, or correct reclosure every time
ResealableAllows a multi-serving package to close again as designedThat the closure was engaged, remains child-resistant, or preserves freshness
Sealed / enclosedContains the product and limits exposure as designedSterility, contaminant-free contents, correct storage, or authentic product
Label intactKeeps required identity and warnings readableThat every claim is true or the lot report matches

New York's current Part 128 guidance is a useful regulated-market example: retail packages must be child-resistant for the product's life, tamper-evident, fully enclosing, sealed against oxygen exposure and contaminants, and resealable for multiple servings. Other jurisdictions and product categories can differ. Always use the responsible regulator for the exact market.

Make the pre-open package record

SELLERExact licensed business · location/domain · transaction time · receipt · staff/order reference
PRODUCTBrand · exact name · form · variant · net quantity/count · expected item
RESPONSIBLE BUSINESSProducer/processor · license · contact route · manufacturer role
TRACEABILITYLot/batch/package ID · manufacture/harvest/package/test/expiration dates
SEALType · location · continuous or broken · alignment · residue · duplicate/overlay · photo
CLOSURECap/latch/zipper/button · child-resistant instructions · first-open indication · condition
CONDITIONLeak · bulge · puncture · stain · corrosion · cracked glass · device damage · temperature concern
LABEL + REPORTWarnings · ingredients · cannabinoid basis · directions · QR · exact-lot CoA · recall search

Take an overall photograph before close-ups. Include every side, seal transition, closure, bottom, label, lot, dates, damage, receipt, and outer delivery packaging when relevant. Keep original files and do not annotate over the only copy.

Inspect without pretending the seal authenticates the product

  1. Compare the complete package.

    Match the order or shelf description, exact format, count, net quantity, and variant. A correctly branded box can still contain the wrong item.

  2. Trace the intended opening path.

    Find every film, band, tab, foil, cap ring, blister, zipper, valve, cartridge plug, or box closure described by the package. Do not assume an unfamiliar seal is missing.

  3. Look for discontinuity.

    Record cuts, lifting, wrinkles, bubbles, adhesive residue, mismatched color, puncture, double label, loose cap, misalignment, or an indicator that already reads opened.

  4. Protect required information.

    A seal should not remove the only lot, warning, symbol, directions, or responsible-business contact. New York specifically says required labeling cannot disappear with the tamper-evident feature.

  5. Check the closure separately.

    Read the exact child-resistant and reclosure instructions. A torn band may show first opening but does not explain how to secure the remaining product.

  6. Stop at uncertainty.

    Ask the licensed seller or manufacturer for a written answer before opening. Do not compare by opening another package you do not intend to use or return.

Run the lot, label, and recall match

  • Match lot and product identity across package, receipt, downloadable certificate, and any recall or quarantine notice.
  • Confirm the report describes the finished product or clearly understand the sample stage and its limits.
  • Keep units, reporting limits, qualifiers, panel scope, report status, and dates attached to results.
  • Verify the exact seller and license through the regulator; a logo, map listing, age gate, or copied license image is not enough.
  • Search current regulator and CPSC recall routes when the concern involves the cannabis product, package, battery, charger, or accessory.
  • Treat “pass,” a QR code, and normal appearance as scoped evidence—not a universal safety guarantee.

The recall checker gives a stop–separate–preserve workflow. The CoA guide keeps sample identity, method, result, unit, limit, panel, and status together.

Use a return or complaint route without destroying the trail

  1. Stabilize danger first.

    Use emergency, Poison Control, fire, battery, or clinical help before contacting customer service.

  2. Preserve the package.

    Keep it closed, isolated, locked, and in the condition the manufacturer or authority directs. Do not combine, clean, recharge, repair, transfer, or discard it merely to simplify a return.

  3. Contact the licensed seller.

    Give neutral observations, exact identifiers, purchase details, photographs, and the remedy requested. Ask how to transport or hand off the item safely and lawfully.

  4. Escalate to the responsible business.

    Use the processor/manufacturer contact printed on the package; keep written instructions and case numbers.

  5. Use the regulator's route.

    New York OCM accepts product, packaging, labeling, business, and adverse-event concerns through its incident process. Other jurisdictions use different routes.

  6. Keep the chain.

    Record who handled the package, when, what was sent, the condition, instructions, return receipt, refund or replacement, and follow-up.

A seller policy is not a safety instruction, and a refund is not a regulator finding. Do not mail cannabis, carry it across a state line, confront a suspected illicit seller, or leave a suspect product in an ordinary public drop box.

Use the checklist before the transaction closes

  • Verify the licensed seller, exact location or domain, and intended product before payment.
  • Read the return, exchange, defect, delivery, and damaged-package policy while the receipt route is still available.
  • Confirm the package has a readable lot, dates, responsible-business contact, storage instructions, warnings, ingredients, and complete-report route required for that market.
  • Inspect visible seal and package condition at handoff without opening it.
  • Keep the receipt and original package; do not transfer the product into an anonymous container.
  • Plan locked storage, first opening, reclosure, and end-of-life handling before bringing the item into a shared household.
