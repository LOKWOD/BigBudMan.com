Cannabis emergency readiness, outage safety and evacuation checklist

The power is out.
The safety plan stays on.

A household emergency can break charging, storage, medicine, pet, transport, and access-control routines at the same time. Pre-decide the safety gates before the lights go out.

General emergency-readiness education—not emergency-management, fire, electrical, battery, medical, legal, insurance, evacuation, or transport advice. Follow emergency officials, 911, utility and fire-department directions, product labels, clinicians, pharmacists, property rules, and current law. Do not delay evacuation to retrieve cannabis or equipment.

THE USEFUL PART

Keep these in your head.

  • Life safety comes first. Evacuate when ordered or when conditions are unsafe; do not delay for cannabis, devices, plants, accessories, records, or a preferred storage container.
  • Keep an offline inventory of exact products, medicines, batteries, chargers, original labels, lot numbers, household access needs, pet risks, and the person responsible for each action.
  • Do not improvise charging, use damaged batteries, defeat alarms, run fuel-burning equipment indoors, block exits, overload temporary power, or store products where children, pets, heat, water, or responders can reach them.
  • An evacuation order does not create permission to cross a state or international border with cannabis. When transport legality or safety is unknown, follow official directions and choose a lawful no-transport or disposal path if available.
  • After the event, quarantine heat-, water-, smoke-, contamination-, seal-, label-, or battery-damaged items. Do not taste, inhale, charge, open, or sell them to decide whether they are safe.

Use the life-safety gate first

!

Leave first when officials order evacuation or conditions are unsafe

Call 911 for fire, smoke, explosion, collapse, trouble breathing, severe injury, inability to awaken, or another immediate threat. For a possible poisoning in the United States, call Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222 or use webPOISONCONTROL. Do not re-enter or delay departure to retrieve a product, device, plant, charger, receipt, or lockbox.

Follow the evacuation route, shelter, utility, fire, police, public-health, and property instructions for the specific incident. This page cannot see the hazard, structural condition, air quality, road closure, local law, or medical need.

Use the evacuation go/no-go card

  1. Go when told.

    People, pets, prescribed emergency medicines and official go-kit items outrank cannabis property. Never re-enter for a forgotten item.

  2. Keep the route lawful.

    An emergency does not erase federal, state, tribal, local, shelter, hotel, landlord, workplace, vehicle, or border rules. Check the destination and route before including any cannabis item.

  3. Keep products inaccessible.

    If lawful transport is confirmed, retain original labeled packaging, protect it from heat and water, and keep it inaccessible to the driver, children, pets and other people.

  4. Separate batteries.

    Power devices off, protect terminals, avoid loose metal contact, and do not pack a damaged, recalled, hot, wet, swollen, leaking, hissing or unidentified battery.

  5. Use a no-transport outcome when needed.

    Follow the regulator, local waste authority, product maker, fire department or recall instruction for lawful disposal. Do not flush, burn, abandon, donate, resell or hide a damaged product.
