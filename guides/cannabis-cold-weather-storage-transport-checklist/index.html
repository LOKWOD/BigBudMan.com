Cold-weather cannabis storage and transport checklist

Cold changes the handoff.
Not the rules.

Plan the lawful trip, preserve the original package, limit the time outside, avoid using a vehicle as storage, let devices return to room temperature, and inspect before opening or charging.

General storage, transport and device-safety education for adults 21+—not legal advice, permission to cross a border or drive impaired, a product warranty, a battery repair method, or a universal temperature specification. Follow the exact package and manufacturer instructions and current state and local law. Keep cannabis locked, inaccessible to children and pets, and never drive impaired.

THE USEFUL PART

Keep these in your head.

  • Keep the sealed original package and label with the product. Add a separate locked container when needed; a lockbox does not make an unlawful trip lawful.
  • A parked vehicle is not a storage cabinet. Temperature swings, access risk, theft and state transport rules remain even when the weather feels protective.
  • Moving a very cold package into warm, humid air can create condensation. Keep it closed during the transition, then inspect the package, seal, contents and device before use.
  • EPA advises storing lithium-ion batteries at room temperature when possible. Do not charge a visibly damaged, swollen, wet, leaking, unusually hot or otherwise abnormal device.
  • The strongest buying decision may be buying nothing: reuse a sound lockable container, shorten the trip, separate a device from the product, or replace a damaged device through the proper route.

Cold weather does not create a legal exception or a sobriety timer. Keep consumption out of the vehicle, keep the driver sober, and verify the rule for the exact place and trip.

Use a closed-package transition, then inspect

  1. Limit exposure.

    Move the package and device between controlled spaces without leaving them in a parked vehicle, snow, direct precipitation or an outdoor bag longer than necessary.

  2. Keep the package closed.

    When a cold item enters warm air, leave the original package closed during the temperature transition. Follow its label rather than inventing a universal wait time.

  3. Inspect the outside first.

    Look for wetness, cracked plastic or glass, lifted seals, leaks, label damage and deformation before opening.

  4. Inspect the product separately.

    After the package is at the condition specified by its label, check appearance, odor, texture and closure for an unexpected change. Do not consume a product that appears contaminated, wet, moldy, damaged or otherwise abnormal.

  5. Document and use the exit route.

    Photograph the package and lot before altering anything, then follow the retailer, manufacturer, regulator, recall or disposal route. Do not dry a questionable product with a heater, oven, microwave or hair dryer.

Give the lithium battery its own safety gate

  • DO NOT CHARGE WHILE VERY COLD OR WETReturn the device to the manufacturer's permitted condition and inspect it first. Never use an external heat source to accelerate the process.
  • STOP FOR DAMAGESwelling, leakage, deformation, torn wrap, exposed contacts, corrosion, unusual odor, hissing, smoke or abnormal heat means stop using and charging the device.
  • ISOLATE, DO NOT IMPROVISEKeep damaged batteries away from people, pets, combustibles and loose metal. Follow local fire-department, household-hazardous-waste or battery-recycling instructions; do not place lithium-ion batteries in household trash or curbside recycling.
  • USE THE SPECIFIED CHARGERA connector that fits is not proof of compatible voltage, current, controls or condition.

EPA says lithium-ion batteries perform best when stored at room temperature and identifies swelling as a sign of damage. CPSC advises consumers to use compatible chargers and stop using recalled or damaged products.
