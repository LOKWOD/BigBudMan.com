THE USEFUL PART

Keep these in your head.

  • Verify that the seller is currently licensed and that the program is permitted and accurately advertised in the relevant jurisdiction before valuing points.
  • Use planned eligible spending—not a target created by the program—and discount headline rewards by the share you realistically expect to redeem before expiry.
  • Subtract membership fees and any documented price or fee difference from usable rewards; a high advertised earn rate can still produce negative net value.
  • Separate age/identity checks required for a lawful transaction from optional rewards enrollment, marketing consent, location access, app permissions, and purchase-history profiling.
  • Keep product, lot, total price, return/complaint route, and responsible-use decisions independent of points, streaks, tiers, countdowns, gifts, or potency-based promotions.

Verify the current rule before the reward math

A seller displaying points does not prove that the offer is permitted, current, or accurately described. Check the responsible cannabis regulator, the exact license, the program terms, and any approval or exception the seller claims.

NY

New York example: adopted rule and proposal are different records

When checked October 22, 2026, New York OCM's adopted Part 129 prohibited cannabis marketing that promotes price reductions, discounts, customer loyalty programs, or coupons except for a packaging-sustainability program or another OCM approval. OCM has also published proposed amendments that would create restricted discount and loyalty allowances. A proposal is not current permission. Verify the adopted rule, effective date, later notices, and any claimed approval before relying on an offer.

This page does not attempt a 50-state rewards audit. The site's weekly legal review owns state-wide status work; this buying tool teaches readers to keep adopted law, pending proposal, seller claim, and personal value in separate columns.

Estimate usable rewards and break-even spend

Use only purchases already planned after the product, seller, price, and safety checks. Enter the program's documented earn rate, then reduce it by the percentage of rewards you honestly expect to redeem before expiry or exclusion. Subtract annual fees and a documented annual price/fee difference versus the best compatible no-program alternative.

Enter planned eligible spend and the documented reward rate. Zero fees are valid only when the terms confirm them.

Headline value = planned eligible spend × reward rate. Usable value = headline value × expected redemption share. Net value = usable value − annual program fee − documented annual price/fee premium. Effective net rate = net value ÷ planned eligible spend. Break-even spend = fee and premium ÷ usable reward rate. Taxes, product eligibility, caps, tiers, point conversion, expiry, returns, delivery, tips, and changing prices remain separate unless explicitly represented in the inputs.

Math check: $600 of already planned eligible spend at 5%, with 75% actually redeemed, creates $30 headline value and $22.50 expected usable value. A $5 annual fee plus a $6 annual price premium leaves $11.50 net value, a 1.92% effective net rate, and $293.33 break-even eligible spend.

Audit the purchase-history profile

A cannabis rewards account can connect identity, contact information, store or location, timestamps, products, purchase frequency, amounts, payment metadata, device/app identifiers, marketing responses, and support messages. That does not prove a program misuses data; it makes the privacy review materially different from collecting a paper punch.

  1. Separate transaction requirements from rewards enrollment.

    Ask what identification is required by law or store policy for the sale, what is copied or retained, and which additional fields exist only for the program.

  2. Read collection and use.

    Identify purchase history, precise location, device IDs, cookies, analytics, profiles, personalization, and any health or preference inferences.

  3. Read sharing and service providers.

    Look for the dispensary, parent company, brands, loyalty vendor, app platform, payment and delivery vendors, analytics, advertisers, data brokers, and legal disclosures.

  4. Test control paths before enrollment.

    Can marketing channels be declined separately? Can location permissions stay off? Can the account be deleted without forfeiting an unresolved refund, recall, or complaint record?

  5. Record retention and incident contacts.

    Find the policy date, retention language, security contact, privacy request route, and breach-notice method. “We value privacy” is not a retention schedule.

The FTC warns that interfaces can bury material terms and fees or steer people toward sharing more data. Read the plain terms and the screen behavior; a prechecked box or brightly emphasized “accept all” button is not evidence of a fair choice.

Keep conversion honest

  1. Verify the licensed seller and current program rule.
  2. Choose the product without points.

    Exact identity, lot evidence, ingredients, label math, storage, and fit come first.

  3. Compare the delivered total.

    Use the same product/package basis and include tax and mandatory fees correctly.

  4. Value only usable rewards.

    Apply expiry, redemption friction, exclusions, and fees; do not increase spending to reach a tier.

  5. Make privacy a separate yes/no decision.

    Declining data collection can be rational even when net dollar value is positive.

  6. Preserve the exit.

    Know how to stop marketing, close the account, use or forfeit points, retrieve records, and handle a return or recall.
