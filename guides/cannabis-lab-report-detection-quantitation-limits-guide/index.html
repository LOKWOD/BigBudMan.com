Cannabis laboratory report detection and quantitation limits guide

“ND” is not zero.
“Detected” is not quantified.

A laboratory qualifier answers a narrower question than most shoppers assume. Read the analyte, method, matrix, unit, detection or quantitation boundary, reported result, acceptance limit, decision rule, and exact sample before turning a row into a claim.

General laboratory-report literacy for adults 21+—not legal advice, medical advice, a product-safety decision, a laboratory audit, or an interpretation of a specific report. Terms and decision rules vary by laboratory, method, matrix, analyte, jurisdiction, report version, and date. Ask the issuing laboratory or responsible regulator when a qualifier, limit, unit, uncertainty statement, or pass/fail decision is unclear.

THE USEFUL PART

Keep these in your head.

  • Not detected usually means the method did not produce a reportable detection under the stated conditions and boundary. It does not prove the analyte is absolutely absent or equal to zero.
  • A detection limit addresses reliable distinction or identification above background. A quantitation limit addresses measurement with acceptable bias and precision. The exact definitions and abbreviations belong to the report, method, and governing program.
  • Below LOQ, estimated, trace, < value, blank, not tested, present, absent, pass, and fail are different statements. Never convert a qualifier into a made-up number for totals, doses, legal thresholds, or risk calculations.
  • A pass decision requires the analyte, result, unit, acceptance limit, rule, and report status to agree. A low reporting limit does not establish that every required panel was tested or that every package in the lot had identical handling.
  • Match the certificate to the exact product and lot, then preserve the laboratory, report number, version, dates, sample identity, methods, units, qualifiers, limits, uncertainty statements, and amendments before comparing reports.

Keep LOD, LOQ, and reporting limit in separate lanes

LOD / DETECTION LIMITA method boundary for reliably distinguishing or identifying an analyte from background under defined conditions
LOQ / LLOQThe lower boundary where a value can be measured with acceptable bias and precision under the method
RL / MRL / REPORTING LIMITThe laboratory or program's reporting boundary; its relationship to LOD and LOQ must be stated, not assumed
ACCEPTANCE LIMITThe regulatory or program threshold used for a decision; not automatically the same as any analytical limit
UNCERTAINTYA quantified statement about dispersion around a measurement result—not permission to choose a preferred number

NIST defines a detection limit around reliable distinction or identification and a quantitation limit around acceptable accuracy or bias and precision. New York's current laboratory quality standard requires laboratories to establish and verify LOD and LOQ under specified procedures and ties the selected LOQ to methodology, reference standards, calibration, matrix, and regulatory limits.

Those concepts are related but not interchangeable. A report may use different abbreviations or set its reporting limit at or above the LOQ. Read the report legend, method, laboratory SOP reference, and regulator guidance before translating a symbol.

Reconstruct a pass or fail decision

  1. Match the right rule.

    Identify the jurisdiction, product category, date, analyte, matrix, unit, and applicable acceptance limit. Do not borrow a threshold from another state or format.

  2. Confirm the method can answer the question.

    Read the method, scope, limit definitions, matrix, dilution or qualification notes, and current report legend.

  3. Keep result and qualifier together.

    Do not strip ND, <LOQ, estimated, over-range, dilution, interference, or other flags from the printed outcome.

  4. Find the decision rule.

    Determine how the program treats rounding, uncertainty, below-limit results, qualitative findings, amended reports, and acceptance limits. Do not invent a margin.

  5. Check final status.

    Draft, preliminary, revised, superseded, canceled, invalidated, and final reports are not interchangeable.

  6. Keep the sample boundary.

    The decision describes the submitted sample and lot process under the program—not every gram, later storage event, or current package condition.

Read measurement uncertainty without using it as a loophole

Measurement uncertainty describes dispersion associated with a measured value under stated conditions. It does not mean that every number inside an interval is equally proven, that a consumer may subtract uncertainty from a result, or that a failing result automatically becomes passing.

New York's 2026 standard gives a narrow reporting example: when a calculated THC or CBD result exceeds 100 percent and the difference is within the laboratory's calculated analytical uncertainty, the laboratory may report 100 percent with a qualifying statement or report the calculated value; the certificate must clearly state the calculated value and uncertainty when qualifying it. That rule is not a general permission to edit results or legal thresholds.

NIST's hemp reference material illustrates the quality-system role of reference values and uncertainty estimates. A laboratory can use a characterized material to check whether its measurement system is performing comparably. A consumer report showing uncertainty still needs the exact lot, method, unit, result, qualifier, decision rule, and report status.

Know when the report needs a qualified reader

  • Ask the issuing laboratory about its codes, method scope, dilution, interference, LOD/LOQ/RL relationship, uncertainty statement, amendments, and whether the displayed report is final.
  • Ask the regulator about required analytes, product-specific limits, decision rules, report access, laboratory authorization, recalls, and complaint routes.
  • Use a qualified clinician or Poison Control for a health or exposure question; a certificate cannot diagnose symptoms, establish a safe personal dose, or replace urgent care.
  • Use a lawyer or responsible agency for a legal threshold, employment, housing, custody, travel, or enforcement question. This guide does not decide how a court or program will treat a result.
  • For severe symptoms, trouble breathing, seizure, collapse, inability to wake, or another emergency, call 911. In the United States, Poison Control is available at 1-800-222-1222 or PoisonHelp.org.
