Cannabis product response, safety and clinical handoff log

Record the response.
Do not run an experiment.

A useful cannabis note preserves the exact product and lot, what happened on a clock, the surrounding context, function, harms, and the right handoff. It does not prove cause, safety, sobriety, or the next amount to use.

Safety and recordkeeping education for adults 21+—not individualized medical advice, a dosing plan, a sobriety test, diagnosis, causation assessment, product trial, or instruction to start, stop, retry, combine, or change cannabis or medicine. Do not delay urgent care to complete a worksheet.

THE USEFUL PART

Keep these in your head.

  • Use the log to document an event that already occurred or to prepare an accurate handoff—not to schedule repeated exposure, increase an amount, or test a dangerous symptom.
  • Preserve the exact seller, product, form, package, producer, lot or batch, label, laboratory-report route, dates, route, clock time, and relevant medicines or substances.
  • Record observable events and function before interpretation. “Vomited twice between 9:10 and 9:35” is more useful than “bad reaction”; neither proves cannabis caused it.
  • Collapse, seizure, trouble breathing, or inability to awaken calls for 911. For possible poisoning in the United States, call Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222 or use webPOISONCONTROL; do not wait for the log.
  • A product response cannot certify fitness to drive or work, establish a safe waiting period, diagnose an allergy or disorder, or transfer to another product, batch, route, day, or person.

Use the safety gate before the form

!

Call 911 for collapse, seizure, trouble breathing, or inability to awaken

For a possible poisoning in the United States, call Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222 or use webPOISONCONTROL. If a child or pet may have been exposed, seek expert help promptly. Do not induce vomiting, give a home remedy, or wait to finish this page.

Chest pain, severe confusion, repeated vomiting, new weakness, severe allergic symptoms, dangerous behavior, pregnancy concerns, medicine interactions, or worsening symptoms need appropriate medical advice. Poison Control and clinicians need the product, amount if known, route, timing, age, symptoms, medicines, and current condition—preserve the package while protecting access.

Use the person's own words where useful. Avoid labels such as “overdose,” “allergy,” “psychosis,” “panic attack,” “interaction,” or “contaminated” unless a qualified source made that determination; record the signs that prompted the concern.

Do not stop or restart prescribed medicine, “counteract” cannabis with another substance, re-expose to confirm a reaction, or change a care plan from this log. Follow the qualified service that has the person's facts.

Turn the private log into the right report

  1. Protect health first.

    Emergency care and Poison Control are response routes, not product-quality investigations.

  2. Preserve the transaction and product.

    Keep receipt, seller, package, lot, label, report route, condition, photos, communications, and custody while preventing access.

  3. Use the owner of the problem.

    The licensed seller or producer, state or tribal cannabis regulator, FDA MedWatch, CPSC, a clinician, a workplace program, and law enforcement answer different questions.

  4. State facts and unknowns.

    Report the event, timing, product and lot, observable harm, actions, and supporting records without declaring contamination, cause, fraud, or a legal violation that has not been established.

  5. Keep the case trail.

    Save submission date, channel, confirmation, case number, requested follow-up, responsible owner, and final disposition.

FDA says consumers and clinicians can use MedWatch to report adverse events involving cannabis and cannabis-derived products, including CBD. FDA reporting does not replace emergency care, a state cannabis complaint, seller contact, or another applicable route.

Keep the record useful and private

  • Store the log where children, visitors, employers, advertisers, shared-device users, and unrelated apps cannot casually access it.
  • Share only the fields the receiving clinician, service, regulator, seller, or program needs; redact account, ID, location, workplace, and third-party details where appropriate.
  • Do not put a package QR code, prescription label, full receipt, face, home interior, or another person's health information in a public post.
  • Keep the original file, date, and unedited evidence when reporting; make a redacted sharing copy rather than overwriting the source.
  • Separate one person's record from another person's response. A shared product does not create a shared medical conclusion.

This log is not a consumption diary leaderboard, dosing calculator, product review, or recommendation engine. More entries do not make intentional re-exposure safe.
