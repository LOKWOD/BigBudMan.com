Cannabis science and labels

THCA and THC
are different rows.

The acid form, the neutral form, and a calculated total answer different questions. The useful move is to keep the analyte, unit, formula, sample, and legal rule attached.

12 min read Reviewed September 8, 2026Adults 21+

ON THIS PAGE

General chemistry and label education—not legal advice, a potency guarantee, or a preparation guide. Cannabis and hemp rules vary by product, jurisdiction, testing basis, and date. Verify the current regulator before possessing, purchasing, transporting, or using a product.

THE USEFUL PART

Keep these in your head.

  • THCA is tetrahydrocannabinolic acid; delta-9 THC is a related neutral cannabinoid. A laboratory may report both separately.
  • Total THC commonly combines measured delta-9 THC with a converted contribution from THCA, but the governing rule and report define the calculation.
  • The 0.877 factor is molecular-mass accounting, not a promise that a device, oven, lighter, body, or homemade process converts every molecule.
  • A legal conclusion requires the applicable product category, jurisdiction, sampling method, dry- or wet-weight basis, measurement uncertainty, and current law—not a front-label phrase.

Three label lines, three meanings

What the familiar 0.877 calculation does

New York's current testing limits define total THC as measured THC plus THCA multiplied by 0.877. USDA hemp-compliance guidance requires a post-decarboxylation or similarly reliable method that considers the potential conversion of THCA into THC. The 0.877 factor reflects the molecular-mass relationship after the acid form loses carbon dioxide.

+

A clearly labeled example

If—and only if—the applicable rule says Total THC = THC + (THCA × 0.877), a report with 1.0% THC and 10.0% THCA would calculate as 1.0 + (10.0 × 0.877) = 9.77% total THC before the rule's rounding and uncertainty treatment. This is label math, not a use instruction or effect forecast.

Do not add total THC to THC or THCA again. Do not apply the example to unlike units, a different product, or a jurisdiction that defines the field differently.

Units and moisture basis can change the comparison

  • Percent by mass: commonly used for plant material, but confirm whether the value is as-received or corrected to dry weight.
  • Milligrams per gram: a mass concentration; do not compare it directly with package milligrams.
  • Milligrams per serving or package: common for finished products; check the serving count and homogeneity rules.
  • Dry-weight basis: excludes moisture from the denominator. USDA hemp compliance uses total THC on a dry-weight basis.
  • Measurement uncertainty: USDA requires laboratories to estimate and report it for official hemp compliance results.

A conversion is valid only when the analytes use compatible units and basis. The CBDA label guide explains the parallel total-CBD issue.

Decarboxylation is chemistry, not a home guarantee

Heat and time can move THCA toward delta-9 THC through decarboxylation, while other changes and losses may occur. Laboratory methods control sample preparation, temperature, time, instruments, calibration, and quality assurance. A consumer setting does not automatically reproduce that system.

The total-THC factor expresses a maximum stoichiometric contribution under a defined calculation. It does not establish the amount delivered by smoking, vaping, cooking, storage, digestion, or any particular device. Temperature at the sample, moisture, particle size, oxygen, duration, prior processing, and degradation can change what happens.

!

Do not turn label math into a recipe

A certificate cannot validate a homemade process, establish a serving, remove fire or inhalation risk, or make an unlicensed product lawful.

Why “THCA hemp” is not a nationwide legal answer

As reviewed September 8, 2026, USDA's official hemp-production testing guidance measures total delta-9 THC on a dry-weight basis and requires methods that consider THCA's potential conversion. State, tribal, federal, finished-product, controlled-substance, food, smokeable-hemp, and retail rules can differ and can change.

  • “THE LABEL SAYS HEMP”A marketing word is not an official compliance sample, license, shipping authorization, or defense under every law.
  • “DELTA-9 IS UNDER 0.3%”The applicable rule may use total THC, dry weight, sampling requirements, and measurement uncertainty.
  • “IT IS LEGAL IN MY STATE”Possession, sale, manufacture, age, product type, local restrictions, and interstate travel remain separate questions.
  • “A COA MAKES IT LEGAL”A mismatched, private, old, or noncompliance report does not replace the regulator's required process.

Use the 50-state law library only as a dated starting point, then open the current state regulator and statute. Never carry cannabis across state or international borders based on a product label.

A certificate check for THCA-heavy products

  1. Match the lot and format.

    Flower, extract, edible, vape, and bulk material are different samples.

  2. Find THCA, delta-9 THC, and total THC.

    Keep result qualifiers, units, and the formula together.

  3. Confirm the basis.

    Look for dry weight, as received, per serving, per package, or another stated basis.

  4. Read the sampling statement.

    A producer-selected retail sample is not automatically an official regulatory compliance sample.

  5. Read measurement uncertainty and reporting limits.

    Borderline results cannot be interpreted responsibly from a rounded headline alone.

  6. Open contaminant results.

    Potency does not prove the absence of microbes, mycotoxins, pesticides, metals, solvents, or foreign material.

  7. Check regulator status.

    Verify the license, recalls, current law, and whether that exact product category may be sold.

The label still cannot predict the person

Total THC helps standardize a chemistry report under a defined method. It does not guarantee how strong a product will feel, when effects begin, how long they last, or whether an adult can drive safely. Route, formulation, amount, frequency, tolerance, other substances, medicines, health, food, and individual biology matter.

Do not use a low displayed delta-9 THC row to dismiss impairment. Do not use a high THCA row to claim a specific effect. Avoid driving, machinery, heights, water, caregiving, or another safety-critical task when impairment may be present. The full label guide puts cannabinoid numbers beside serving, ingredients, warnings, batch, and dates.
