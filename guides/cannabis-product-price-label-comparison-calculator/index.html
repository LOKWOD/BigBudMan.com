Cannabis price and label worksheet

Normalize the package.
Not the hype.

A menu price is not the delivered total, and a larger package is not automatically better value. Put three compatible products on one worksheet, include mandatory taxes and fees consistently, then keep arithmetic separate from product evidence, usable quantity, storage, dates, safety, and the valid choice to buy nothing.

Independent shopping arithmetic—not a product recommendation, dose calculator, potency target, legal conclusion, price quote, or evidence that a lower cost is a better or safer purchase. Compare only lawful, verified products of the same format and label basis. Prices, taxes, availability, labels, and rules can change.

THE USEFUL PART

Keep these in your head.

  • Compare only candidates with the same product format, route, net-quantity unit, cannabinoid basis, tax convention, shopping moment, and evidence standard.
  • Checkout total equals the entered product price plus entered mandatory tax and fees. Cost per unit, net-quantity unit, or 100 labeled milligrams is arithmetic—not a potency target, quality grade, dose, or personal-value score.
  • A larger or cheaper package can lose value when some of it will expire, cannot be stored securely, does not fit the intended use, or fails the lot, ingredient, condition, recall, or return gate.
  • The calculator runs locally, preserves blanks as unavailable results, and does not send or save entries. A valid result is no purchase.

Treat package fit and likely waste as a separate decision

The calculator intentionally does not ask for an estimated “usable percentage.” That number can become an invented shelf-life, consumption, or dose assumption. Instead, write a yes/no record before accepting a bulk discount:

  • The intended adult wants this exact format and package amount—not merely the lowest ratio.
  • The package can remain in its original identity and required condition for the full intended period.
  • The entire package fits the locked household storage system and access routine.
  • The printed expiration/use-by/opening instruction and actual shopping date leave a realistic window without extending a deadline.
  • Dividing or transferring the package is not required to make the purchase fit.
  • The return, complaint, recall and disposal routes are known.

If any answer is no or unknown, use the smaller compatible package or no-purchase outcome even when its normalized cost is higher. The product dates and condition guide keeps expiration wording, opening state, storage and condition in one record.

Keep the non-price gates beside the arithmetic

  1. Legal source.

    Verify the exact seller, address, and channel through the responsible regulator. A copied logo or map pin is not a license check.

  2. Exact identity.

    Match producer, product, format, count, net quantity, ingredients, lot, and dates across package, receipt, and certificate.

  3. Complete evidence.

    Open the full, final certificate for the finished product and exact lot; check status, panels, units, qualifiers, and report date.

  4. Format fit.

    Route, onset, duration, ability to divide a serving, household storage, medicines, driving, workplace obligations, and prior reactions can matter more than a cost ratio.

  5. Condition and instructions.

    Inspect the sealed package, warnings, storage, child-resistance instructions, device compatibility when applicable, and current recall notices.

  6. Complaint and return route.

    Keep the receipt, package, report link, and seller information. Know who handles a defect, adverse event, or recall.

Do not turn cost per milligram into a potency contest

The per-100-milligram result is optional because many comparisons do not support it. It can show how two like-for-like packages price the same labeled total, but it cannot show the amount a person absorbs, the effect of a puff or bite, laboratory uncertainty, manufacturing consistency, contaminants, product condition, or whether the product fits a person's risk profile.

Higher labeled potency can increase the chance of unwanted or harmful effects. It is not a quality grade. A lower cost per labeled milligram may simply purchase more intoxicating material than the reader intends to use or store. Keep the desired format and the complete label decision ahead of the arithmetic.

Take the ratio from the calculator and the evidence from the record. Neither one can substitute for the other.

Use the worksheet as a pause point, not a winner badge

  • Replace A, B, and C with exact product identifiers before deciding.
  • Save the shopping timestamp and price boundary; an old result is not a current quote.
  • Enter zero only when the receipt shows a real zero. Keep unknown fields blank.
  • Reject comparisons across routes, quantity units, cannabinoids, label bases, jurisdictions, or evidence standards.
  • Choose the package that fits secure storage, date/condition limits, intended amount, return rights, and the no-purchase alternative—not merely the lowest ratio.
  • If the least expensive result fails a source, lot, ingredient, condition, recall, storage, or exit gate, remove it from the comparison.
