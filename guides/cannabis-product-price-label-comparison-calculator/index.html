Cannabis price and label worksheet

Normalize the package.
Not the hype.

Package price hides count, net quantity, and label basis. Put three like-for-like products on the same worksheet, then keep cost arithmetic separate from quality, safety, route, and personal fit.

11 min read Reviewed September 26, 2026Adults 21+

ON THIS PAGE

Independent shopping arithmetic—not a product recommendation, dose calculator, potency target, legal conclusion, price quote, or evidence that a lower cost is a better or safer purchase. Compare only lawful, verified products of the same format and label basis. Prices, taxes, availability, labels, and rules can change.

THE USEFUL PART

Keep these in your head.

  • Compare like with like: the same product format, quantity unit, cannabinoid basis, tax treatment, and point in time.
  • Cost per unit and cost per gram or milliliter normalize package size. Cost per 100 labeled milligrams only normalizes a stated label total; it does not measure delivered dose, effect, purity, or value.
  • A valid shortlist still needs licensed seller verification, exact lot identity, a complete certificate, ingredients, dates, storage, recall checks, and return or complaint routes.
  • The calculator runs in the browser, starts at zero, accepts blanks safely, and does not send or save the entries.

Choose a comparison basis before entering numbers

FieldUse it whenDo not infer
Receipt priceEvery product uses the same tax and discount convention from the same shopping moment.Current availability, a permanent price, or a genuine discount.
Unit countThe package contains discrete pieces, capsules, pre-rolls, or other comparable units.Each unit is identical, accurately filled, or one personal serving.
Net quantityEvery product uses the same unit, such as grams or milliliters.Equal net quantity means equal potency, quality, freshness, or effect.
Total labeled cannabinoid mgThe package clearly states the same cannabinoid and total-package basis for every compared item.Delivered dose, absorption, impairment, medical value, or a safe amount.

Do not put flower percentage beside edible milligrams or milliliters beside grams and call the result normalized. A package may report THC, THCA, total THC, CBD, or another cannabinoid on different bases. Use the label guide and THCA versus THC guide before entering a total.

Keep the non-price gates beside the arithmetic

  1. Legal source.

    Verify the exact seller, address, and channel through the responsible regulator. A copied logo or map pin is not a license check.

  2. Exact identity.

    Match producer, product, format, count, net quantity, ingredients, lot, and dates across package, receipt, and certificate.

  3. Complete evidence.

    Open the full, final certificate for the finished product and exact lot; check status, panels, units, qualifiers, and report date.

  4. Format fit.

    Route, onset, duration, ability to divide a serving, household storage, medicines, driving, workplace obligations, and prior reactions can matter more than a cost ratio.

  5. Condition and instructions.

    Inspect the sealed package, warnings, storage, child-resistance instructions, device compatibility when applicable, and current recall notices.

  6. Complaint and return route.

    Keep the receipt, package, report link, and seller information. Know who handles a defect, adverse event, or recall.

Do not turn cost per milligram into a potency contest

The per-100-milligram result is optional because many comparisons do not support it. It can show how two like-for-like packages price the same labeled total, but it cannot show the amount a person absorbs, the effect of a puff or bite, laboratory uncertainty, manufacturing consistency, contaminants, product condition, or whether the product fits a person's risk profile.

Higher labeled potency can increase the chance of unwanted or harmful effects. It is not a quality grade. A lower cost per labeled milligram may simply purchase more intoxicating material than the reader intends to use or store. Keep the desired format and the complete label decision ahead of the arithmetic.

Use the worksheet as a pause point, not a winner badge

  • Write the exact product name rather than A, B, or C before making a decision.
  • Save the receipt price and timestamp; do not preserve an old result as a current quote.
  • Mark any unknown as unknown instead of copying a menu number into a package field.
  • Reject comparisons across different routes, quantity units, cannabinoids, or label bases.
  • Print or save the worksheet with the package and certificate notes when a purchase is material.
  • If the cheapest result fails a source, lot, ingredient, storage, condition, or recall gate, it is not the best value.
