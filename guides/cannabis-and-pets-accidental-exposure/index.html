Pet exposure emergency action guide

Your pet reached cannabis.
Make the call useful.

Separate the animal from the source, call promptly, and hand the veterinarian one honest record of the product, co-ingredients, time window, missing amount, signs, and hardware.

Urgent pet-safety education—not veterinary diagnosis, a dose calculator, or treatment. If exposure is possible, contact your veterinarian, an emergency veterinary hospital, or a recognized animal poison-control service now. Use emergency care for severe or rapidly worsening signs. Do not delay the call to complete this guide.

THE USEFUL PART

Keep these in your head.

  • Separate the pet from the product and call promptly; do not wait for a complete symptom list or try to prove a dose first.
  • Report species, weight, health conditions, time window, route, amount that may be missing, exact cannabinoids, all ingredients, and every observed change.
  • Edibles can add chocolate, xylitol, raisins, caffeine, alcohol, or high fat; cartridges, batteries, glass, wrappers, and desiccant packets add non-cannabis hazards.
  • Do not induce vomiting or give food, milk, oil, charcoal, salt, supplements, CBD, or human medicine unless a veterinary professional directs it for that animal.
  • Close the incident with a locked-storage, waste, guest-bag, and after-use check that prevents the same access route from reopening.

The no-delay response card

1 · STOP ACCESSMove the pet away · secure remaining product, crumbs, waste, liquid, ash, hardware, batteries, and wrappers
2 · CALLRegular veterinarian · emergency veterinary hospital · recognized animal poison-control service
3 · IDENTIFYExact product · package photos · THC/CBD per unit and package · ingredients · lot · source
4 · BOUND THE EXPOSURESpecies · weight · age · conditions · earliest/latest time · amount possibly missing · route
5 · REPORT SIGNSAlertness · balance · pupils · trembling · vomiting · urination · breathing · heart concerns · seizures
6 · MOVE SAFELYPrevent falls, stairs, water, heat, traffic, and further access · use a sober driver

In the United States, ASPCA Animal Poison Control lists (888) 426-4435, 24 hours a day, and notes that a consultation fee may apply. Call the veterinary service that can take responsibility for the individual case. A webpage cannot decide whether home observation, examination, decontamination, monitoring, or hospital care is appropriate.

Do not spend the first minutes calculating milligrams from a partial wrapper, searching for a strain effect, or waiting for the pet to look more impaired. Give the best range you have and say which facts are unknown.

Signs and product details that raise urgency

Veterinary references describe possible THC-toxicosis signs such as poor coordination, lethargy, trembling, vomiting, urinary incontinence, unusual responses to sound or touch, and changes in heart rate or body temperature. Those signs are not unique to cannabis, and absence of one sign does not clear an exposure.

  • TROUBLE BREATHING, COLLAPSE, SEIZURE, OR INABILITY TO WAKEUse an emergency veterinary service immediately.
  • RAPID WORSENING, REPEATED VOMITING, OR INABILITY TO STANDPrevent falls and get professional direction now.
  • CHOCOLATE, XYLITOL, RAISINS, CAFFEINE, ALCOHOL, OR MEDICINEThe non-cannabis ingredient can create its own toxicology emergency.
  • CARTRIDGE, BATTERY, GLASS, WRAPPER, OR DESICCANT MISSINGChewing or swallowing hardware and packaging adds burn, leakage, injury, and obstruction concerns.
  • UNKNOWN PRODUCT, AMOUNT, OR TIMEUncertainty is information to report, not a reason to wait.
  • VERY SMALL, VERY YOUNG, ELDERLY, PREGNANT, OR MEDICALLY FRAGILE ANIMALState the animal's size and conditions at the start of the call.

Do not label the pet “just high.” A veterinarian may need to consider other exposures, illness, trauma, glucose or temperature problems, or neurologic causes too.

Keep the package and remaining material away from the pet, children, and other animals. Bring them only as the veterinary service directs. If collecting evidence risks a leaking battery, broken glass, unknown chemical, or another exposure, stop and describe it instead.

A timeline helps the veterinary team see change. It does not prove which ingredient caused a sign, predict outcome, or create permission to watch an animal without professional direction.

Skip improvised home treatment

Do not make the animal vomit or give hydrogen peroxide, salt, mustard, oil, milk, food, activated charcoal, supplements, CBD, or human medicine unless a veterinarian or animal poison specialist gives a specific instruction for that pet. An animal that is sleepy, uncoordinated, seizing, or unable to protect its airway can be harmed by an attempted oral remedy.

Do not walk a wobbly animal to “sober it up,” place it in a bath, expose it to cold, or use internet dose charts. Keep the pet in a quiet, safe area while arranging care. Treatment decisions depend on the exposure, timing, symptoms, species, size, and medical history.

Tell the veterinarian exactly what you know

Concealing cannabis because of embarrassment or legal concern can waste time and send the diagnostic work in the wrong direction. Say whether the item came from a licensed dispensary, hemp shop, home preparation, prescription bottle, unmarked container, or unknown source. Report alcohol, medicines, nicotine, mushrooms, or other possible exposures too.

Keep uncertainty visible: “one 5 mg piece may be missing” is different from “the dog ate 5 mg.” If packaging is unavailable, describe the container, form, ingredients you remember, and who may know more. A photograph of the remaining item can help, but it does not replace professional assessment.

Inhalation and residue are separate hazards

Pets should not be kept in a room where cannabis is smoked or aerosolized. Move an exposed animal to fresh air when it is safe, stop the source, and contact a veterinarian for coughing, breathing difficulty, marked lethargy, collapse, or other concerning signs. An air cleaner is not permission to expose an animal indoors.

Ash, roaches, reclaim, rinse liquid, used wipes, edible crumbs, vape residue, and trash can remain accessible after the adult considers the session finished. Put every product and waste item into secure storage or disposal immediately. The indoor-air guide covers source control; the storage guide covers the lock-and-label system.

CBD marketed for pets is not automatically approved or harmless

FDA says it has not approved CBD products for use in animals and advises owners to discuss treatment options with a veterinarian. A pet label, natural claim, hemp source, spectrum badge, or certificate of analysis does not establish an approved veterinary indication, correct dose, absence of THC, ingredient safety, or freedom from interactions.

Do not use a human CBD product to treat a pet, substitute it for prescribed veterinary care, or assume an accidental exposure is minor because the package says CBD. Bring the complete product record to the veterinarian. The CBD spectrum guide explains why retail category words cannot guarantee composition.

Close the exact access route

  1. Keep the regulated package and identity.

    Product name, cannabinoids, ingredients, lot, dates, warnings, and emergency information should remain attached.

  2. Put every product inside a locked outer container.

    A high shelf, closed door, zipped bag, child-resistant cap, or pet-resistant claim is not a consistently engaged lock.

  3. Separate cannabis from food, pet medicine, and human medicine.

    Do not refrigerate an edible beside ordinary snacks or keep an oil beside pet supplements.

  4. Control the use zone and waste.

    Count pieces, inspect floors and upholstery, cool and contain ash, cap liquids, secure cartridges, and close trash before the pet returns.

  5. Include guests, vehicles, and deliveries.

    Purses, coats, suitcases, bedside tables, door pockets, delivery bags, and an open trunk can bypass the household system.

  6. Post current contacts.

    Save the regular veterinarian, nearest emergency hospital, chosen poison-control service, route, hours, and transport plan before an incident.

  7. Audit the failure after an exposure.

    Name the product, access path, failed layer, correction, responsible adult, and completion date.

The storage system guide covers format-specific conditions. The package-and-lock guide separates child-resistant packaging, placement, lock, and household routine.
