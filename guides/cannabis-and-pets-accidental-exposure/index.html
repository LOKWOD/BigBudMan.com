Pet safety and responsible use

Your pet found cannabis.
Call before guessing.

A chewed gummy bag, spilled flower, used cartridge, or unexplained wobble is a veterinary question. Preserve the evidence, tell the truth, and get case-specific help promptly.

12 min read Reviewed September 9, 2026Adults 21+

ON THIS PAGE

Urgent pet-safety education—not veterinary diagnosis or treatment. If exposure is possible, contact your veterinarian or an animal poison-control service now; use an emergency veterinary hospital for severe or rapidly worsening signs. Do not wait for this page to identify the product or dose.

THE USEFUL PART

Keep these in your head.

  • Call a veterinarian or animal poison-control service promptly; honest product and timing details help more than a home diagnosis.
  • Keep the original package, ingredient list, lot, remaining amount, and photos, but do not delay care to create a perfect record.
  • Edibles can add hazards such as chocolate, xylitol, raisins, caffeine, or concentrated fat; hardware and packaging can create obstruction or battery risks.
  • Do not induce vomiting, give food, milk, oil, activated charcoal, or a human medicine unless a veterinary professional specifically directs it for that animal.

The first five minutes

  1. Separate the pet from the source.

    Move remaining product, wrappers, liquid, ash, plant material, cartridges, batteries, and cleaning waste out of reach without exposing yourself to a leaking battery or unknown chemical.

  2. Call for case-specific advice.

    Contact the pet's veterinarian, an emergency veterinary hospital, or an animal poison-control service. In the United States, ASPCA Animal Poison Control lists (888) 426-4435 and notes that a consultation fee may apply.

  3. State what may have happened.

    Give the animal's species, approximate weight, age, health conditions, symptoms, possible product, amount missing, exposure route, and earliest possible time. Say plainly when a detail is unknown.

  4. Preserve the evidence.

    Bring or photograph the package, ingredient panel, cannabinoid amount, serving and package totals, brand, lot, remaining pieces, and any chewed hardware. Do not risk another exposure while collecting it.

  5. Follow the professional's transport instructions.

    Keep the animal quiet and away from stairs, water, traffic, heat, and other pets. Do not drive if you are impaired; arrange a sober driver or emergency transport.

Signs that make emergency care especially important

Cannabis exposure can resemble other poisonings, neurologic problems, metabolic illness, trauma, or medication effects. Veterinary sources describe possible signs including unusual sleepiness or agitation, poor coordination, trembling, exaggerated responses to sound or touch, drooling, vomiting, urinary leakage, changes in heart rate or body temperature, seizures, and coma.

  • TROUBLE BREATHING, COLLAPSE, SEIZURE, OR UNRESPONSIVENESSUse an emergency veterinary service immediately.
  • RAPID WORSENING OR INABILITY TO STANDPrevent falls and get professional direction now.
  • UNKNOWN EDIBLE OR MULTIPLE INGREDIENTSChocolate, xylitol, raisins, caffeine, and other ingredients can change the emergency.
  • CARTRIDGE, BATTERY, GLASS, OR WRAPPER MISSINGSwallowing or chewing hardware adds obstruction, burn, leakage, and injury concerns.
  • VERY SMALL, VERY YOUNG, ELDERLY, PREGNANT, OR MEDICALLY FRAGILE ANIMALThe margin for waiting may be smaller; tell the veterinary professional.

A pet looking merely “high” is not a safe triage conclusion. Do not wait for a complete symptom list before calling.

Skip improvised home treatment

Do not make the animal vomit or give hydrogen peroxide, salt, mustard, oil, milk, food, activated charcoal, supplements, CBD, or human medicine unless a veterinarian or animal poison specialist gives a specific instruction for that pet. An animal that is sleepy, uncoordinated, seizing, or unable to protect its airway can be harmed by an attempted oral remedy.

Do not walk a wobbly animal to “sober it up,” place it in a bath, expose it to cold, or use internet dose charts. Keep the pet in a quiet, safe area while arranging care. Treatment decisions depend on the exposure, timing, symptoms, species, size, and medical history.

Tell the veterinarian exactly what you know

Concealing cannabis because of embarrassment or legal concern can waste time and send the diagnostic work in the wrong direction. Say whether the item came from a licensed dispensary, hemp shop, home preparation, prescription bottle, unmarked container, or unknown source. Report alcohol, medicines, nicotine, mushrooms, or other possible exposures too.

Keep uncertainty visible: “one 5 mg piece may be missing” is different from “the dog ate 5 mg.” If packaging is unavailable, describe the container, form, ingredients you remember, and who may know more. A photograph of the remaining item can help, but it does not replace professional assessment.

Inhalation and residue are separate hazards

Pets should not be kept in a room where cannabis is smoked or aerosolized. Move an exposed animal to fresh air when it is safe, stop the source, and contact a veterinarian for coughing, breathing difficulty, marked lethargy, collapse, or other concerning signs. An air cleaner is not permission to expose an animal indoors.

Ash, roaches, reclaim, rinse liquid, used wipes, edible crumbs, vape residue, and trash can remain accessible after the adult considers the session finished. Put every product and waste item into secure storage or disposal immediately. The indoor-air guide covers source control; the storage guide covers the lock-and-label system.

CBD marketed for pets is not automatically approved or harmless

FDA says it has not approved CBD products for use in animals and advises owners to discuss treatment options with a veterinarian. A pet label, natural claim, hemp source, spectrum badge, or certificate of analysis does not establish an approved veterinary indication, correct dose, absence of THC, ingredient safety, or freedom from interactions.

Do not use a human CBD product to treat a pet, substitute it for prescribed veterinary care, or assume an accidental exposure is minor because the package says CBD. Bring the complete product record to the veterinarian. The CBD spectrum guide explains why retail category words cannot guarantee composition.

Close the household access loop

  1. Keep original packaging.

    Identity, cannabinoid amounts, ingredients, lot, and emergency information should stay with the product.

  2. Use a locked outer container.

    A high shelf, zipped bag, closed bedroom, child-resistant cap, or pet-proof claim is not the same as a consistently engaged lock.

  3. Separate from food and medicine.

    Do not refrigerate an edible beside ordinary snacks or keep a tincture beside pet medicine.

  4. Count and inspect after use.

    Look for dropped pieces, crumbs, spilled liquid, hot ash, damaged hardware, and open trash before the pet re-enters.

  5. Brief guests and caregivers.

    Visitors' bags, coats, vehicles, purses, and bedside tables can bypass the household system.

  6. Post the contacts.

    Save the regular veterinarian, nearest emergency hospital, and chosen animal poison-control number before an incident.
