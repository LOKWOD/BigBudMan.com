Urgent cannabis safety guide

Too much cannabis?
Use a real response plan.

Do not guess from a meme, force a home remedy, or wait for a child to look worse. Separate emergencies from poison-center questions and preserve the product evidence.

12 min read Reviewed September 20, 2026Adults 21+

ON THIS PAGE

General public-health education for adults 21+—not diagnosis or individualized treatment. Call 911 for collapse, seizure, trouble breathing, inability to wake, severe confusion, dangerous behavior, chest pain, or another emergency. In the United States, contact Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222 or Poison.org for a suspected exposure.

THE USEFUL PART

Keep these in your head.

  • Call 911 for an immediate emergency; do not drive an impaired, faint, severely confused, or medically unstable person yourself.
  • For a suspected exposure that is not yet a 911 emergency, contact Poison Control promptly instead of waiting for symptoms or improvising treatment.
  • Keep the original package and record product, THC amount, serving size, estimated quantity, time, route, age, weight, symptoms, medicines, and other substances.
  • Do not induce vomiting, give another intoxicant, use caffeine as an antidote, or rely on a shower, food, sleep, or internet timing chart to prove safety.
  • Children and pets need separate professional guidance; a small-looking edible can contain multiple adult servings and delayed effects can worsen after discovery.

Start with the emergency gate

!

Call 911 now for emergency warning signs

Collapse, seizure, trouble breathing, inability to wake, severe confusion, dangerous or violent behavior, chest pain, blue or gray color, serious injury, or another rapidly worsening condition needs emergency services. Stay with the person, follow the dispatcher, and do not make them walk, drive, shower alone, or “sleep it off.”

If the situation is not clearly a 911 emergency, contact Poison Control online or at 1-800-222-1222. The national service is free and confidential. New York lists the same number for its regional poison centers. Calling early lets a specialist use the exact person, product, amount, route, and timing instead of a generic internet estimate.

Do not wait for a child, older adult, first-time consumer, or person who mixed substances to “act high.” Effects can be delayed, the package amount may be unclear, and another medical problem may resemble cannabis intoxication.

Build the exposure record before details disappear

PERSONAge, approximate weight, medical conditions, pregnancy possibility, and current medicines
PRODUCTProducer, exact name, format, ingredients, THC/CBD per serving and package, lot, and source
AMOUNTBest estimate consumed, missing, inhaled, spilled, or transferred; number of pieces or servings
TIME + ROUTEWhen exposure occurred and whether swallowed, inhaled, absorbed, or unknown
OTHER SUBSTANCESAlcohol, prescriptions, over-the-counter drugs, supplements, or other intoxicants
SYMPTOMSOnset and changes in alertness, breathing, balance, speech, heart symptoms, vomiting, or behavior

Photograph all sides of the package, the ingredient list, lot, receipt, and any remaining pieces. Bring the package to care when safe. Do not throw away, repackage, wash, charge, heat, or sample the product while professionals are trying to identify it.

Skip the fake antidotes

  • DO NOT INDUCE VOMITINGDo not use fingers, salt water, syrup, or another home method unless a poison specialist specifically directs it.
  • DO NOT ADD ALCOHOL OR DRUGSAlcohol, sedatives, stimulants, nicotine, or another cannabis product can complicate symptoms and evaluation.
  • DO NOT FORCE FOOD OR FLUIDA very drowsy, vomiting, choking, or confused person may not swallow safely. Follow professional instructions.
  • DO NOT USE CAFFEINE AS AN ANTIDOTECoffee and energy drinks do not neutralize THC and can add heart-rate, anxiety, or dehydration concerns.
  • DO NOT USE A COLD SHOWERWater does not remove THC; an unsteady or confused person can fall, faint, aspirate, or be left unsupervised.
  • DO NOT TRUST A COUNTDOWNProduct, amount, route, biology, medicines, and co-exposures vary. A timer cannot certify recovery or driving fitness.

While help is being arranged

Move away from traffic, water, stairs, balconies, cooking, flames, machinery, and driving. Keep the environment calm and reduce noise and crowding. A sober adult should remain present and watch for changes in breathing, alertness, vomiting, color, balance, chest symptoms, or behavior. Follow the poison specialist or emergency dispatcher rather than a prewritten checklist when their instructions differ.

Do not restrain a person unless needed to prevent immediate harm and directed by trained responders. If the person becomes unresponsive but is breathing, follow dispatcher guidance for positioning and monitoring. If breathing stops or there is no normal breathing, call 911 and begin the dispatcher-directed response. Never put food, drink, or an object in the mouth of someone having a seizure.

Keep children and pets away from the remaining product and cleanup area. A pet exposure belongs with a veterinarian or animal poison service; the human Poison Control line does not replace veterinary assessment.

Separate one exposure from recurring patterns

SituationWhat it may meanBest next lane
Delayed edible effectsMore was taken before the first serving was fully felt.Poison Control or medical care based on symptoms; then review serving and storage decisions.
Unknown product or unlabeled bagDose, ingredients, contaminants, and source cannot be verified.Stop use, preserve evidence, contact Poison Control or care, and report through the regulator when appropriate.
Repeated vomiting over episodesThe pattern may need evaluation for CHS or another cause.Use the CHS warning-sign guide and clinical evaluation; do not self-diagnose.
Repeated panic, confusion, falls, or missed obligationsThe product, amount, setting, medicines, or use pattern is not working safely.Stop, discuss the pattern with a clinician, and use substance-use support when needed.
Child or pet accessThe storage system failed even if the package was resealed.Professional exposure guidance first, then locked storage and a household access audit.

An unpleasant experience is not proof of permanent harm, but it is useful evidence. Record what happened before resuming use. A lower label number, different strain name, or new package color does not automatically solve the product, context, medication, or access problem.

Close the loop after the immediate event

  1. Keep the original package.

    Serving math, ingredients, lot, warnings, and emergency information need to remain attached.

  2. Separate one serving before use.

    Do not eat from a multi-serving package or pour an unknown amount into another container.

  3. Wait for the format.

    Edibles and beverages can be delayed and unpredictable; adding more early compounds uncertainty.

  4. Do not mix.

    Avoid alcohol and other intoxicants, and discuss medication interactions with a pharmacist or clinician.

  5. Lock every product.

    Use a locked outer container that children and visitors cannot access; “high up” and “hidden” are not locks.

  6. Plan transport and supervision.

    No driving, childcare, water activity, cooking, tools, or machinery after consuming.

Use the edible timing guide, label guide, and storage guide to repair the specific failure instead of relying on willpower or memory.
