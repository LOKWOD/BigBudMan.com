Use the source
that can act.

A federal health page cannot verify a state license. A state regulator cannot replace emergency care. A laboratory report cannot resolve fraud. Start with the question, then use the agency or service that actually owns it.

U.S. consumer source map—not medical or legal advice, an emergency service, a complete agency directory, or a promise that an agency will investigate an individual report. Websites, jurisdiction, programs, and reporting routes can change. Use 911 for an immediate life threat and Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222 for a U.S. poisoning question.

THE USEFUL PART

Keep these in your head.

  • Choose by job: emergency response, health evidence, treatment support, product regulation, state licensing, recalls, fraud, driving, employment testing, travel, mailing, and hemp production are different lanes.
  • Start at the agency's own domain. A search result, screenshot, social post, AI summary, QR code, retailer page, or copied government seal is not the source record.
  • Preserve the exact product, package, lot, dates, receipt, page URL, screenshots, messages, direct observations, and any case number. Do not send passwords, payment-card details, authentication codes, or unrelated medical records.
  • An official page can still have a narrow scope or an old publication date. Record what the source controls, when you checked it, and which questions remain unresolved.

Care comes before evidence collection. If safe, keep the package available for responders, but do not delay a call to photograph, calculate, search, induce vomiting, drive an impaired person, or confront someone who is agitated or disconnected from reality.

These pages summarize evidence and federal product responsibilities. They do not diagnose a person, authenticate a seller, establish a state's possession law, or prove that the item in hand contains what its label says. For individual decisions, bring the exact product and medication record to an appropriate clinician or pharmacist.

A single event can need more than one lane. A health event, mislabeled package, unsafe accessory, unlicensed seller, and payment scam remain separate claims even when they occurred in the same purchase.

Let state and tribal authorities answer state and tribal questions

  1. Identify every jurisdiction.

    Possession, purchase, delivery, use, driving, employment, medical enrollment, cultivation, housing, airport, federal-property, and tribal rules may come from different authorities.

  2. Open the regulator record.

    Use the state or tribal agency's current license, recall, product, patient, consumer, and enforcement pages. The site's 50-state law library is a dated route to those sources, not a substitute for them.

  3. Verify the exact seller and product.

    Match legal name, address, license status, website, product producer, lot, certificate, and receipt. A regulator logo or social badge is not a license lookup.

  4. Check the date and legal status.

    Separate enacted law, effective rule, emergency action, proposed rule, court order, guidance, FAQ, press release, and pending bill.

  5. Ask a qualified professional when consequences matter.

    Official public pages cannot decide an individualized criminal, employment, immigration, custody, housing, medical, or benefits question.

Run the six-part source test

  1. Owner:

    Does the agency or service actually control this question?

  2. Jurisdiction:

    Does the page cover the right country, state, tribe, property, program, product, and person?

  3. Status:

    Is it law, regulation, formal order, guidance, FAQ, research summary, recall, enforcement notice, or reporting portal?

  4. Date:

    When was it published, reviewed, amended, effective, or accessed?

  5. Object:

    Does it address the exact product, lot, license, job category, itinerary, shipment, or symptom question?

  6. Exit:

    What official route handles an emergency, correction, complaint, appeal, treatment search, or professional question?

If one field is unknown, write “unknown.” Do not fill a jurisdictional gap with a retailer blog, copied chart, influencer post, forum answer, or search snippet.
