U.S. consumer source map—not medical or legal advice, an emergency service, a complete agency directory, or a promise that an agency will investigate an individual report. Websites, jurisdiction, programs, and reporting routes can change. Use 911 for an immediate life threat and Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222 for a U.S. poisoning question.

THE USEFUL PART

Keep these in your head.

  • Route the person before the paperwork: 911 for immediate danger, Poison Control for a U.S. poisoning question, and 988 for a U.S. mental-health crisis.
  • Split one event into separate lanes. Health care, state cannabis regulation, FDA product reporting, consumer-product recalls, payment fraud, employment, travel, and law can have different owners.
  • Record owner, jurisdiction, source type, exact object, status, dates, direct URL, conclusion, limits, and next route. A government domain does not make a page current or relevant to every question.
  • Preserve the smallest useful evidence packet. Do not delay care, publish personal data, or send passwords, authentication codes, payment-card details, or unrelated medical records.

A single event can need more than one lane. A health event, mislabeled package, unsafe accessory, unlicensed seller, and payment scam remain separate claims even when they occurred in the same purchase.

Let state and tribal authorities answer state and tribal questions

  1. Identify every jurisdiction.

    Possession, purchase, delivery, use, driving, employment, medical enrollment, cultivation, housing, airport, federal-property, and tribal rules may come from different authorities.

  2. Open the regulator record.

    Use the state or tribal agency's current license, recall, product, patient, consumer, and enforcement pages. The site's 50-state law library is a dated route to those sources, not a substitute for them.

  3. Verify the exact seller and product.

    Match legal name, address, license status, website, product producer, lot, certificate, and receipt. A regulator logo or social badge is not a license lookup.

  4. Check the date and legal status.

    Separate enacted law, effective rule, emergency action, proposed rule, court order, guidance, FAQ, press release, and pending bill.

  5. Ask a qualified professional when consequences matter.

    Official public pages cannot decide an individualized criminal, employment, immigration, custody, housing, medical, or benefits question.

Build a one-page source handoff

DECISIONOne question the next person can answer; split health, product, seller, accessory, payment, work, travel, and law
OWNER + JURISDICTIONAgency, service, regulator, court, legislature, employer, clinician, manufacturer; country, state, tribe, property, program, or job
SOURCE TYPEStatute, regulation, order, license record, recall, reporting portal, official guidance, evidence review, or support service
EXACT OBJECTPerson, product, lot, model, seller, license, address, policy, itinerary, shipment, or claim
STATUS + DATESCurrent, archived, proposed, pending, superseded, effective, amended, reviewed, and accessed
DIRECT SOURCEOfficial owner, page title, full URL, relevant section, saved copy when appropriate
CONCLUSION + LIMITShort paraphrase; what the source establishes, what it does not, and unresolved facts
NEXT ROUTECare, complaint, appeal, correction, regulator, counsel, employer process, or dated recheck

The handoff is designed to travel: from consumer to clinician, regulator, attorney, employer, manufacturer, poison specialist, or another researcher. Attach only the evidence that route needs. Redact account credentials and unrelated personal information before sharing.

Run the eight-part source test

  1. Owner:

    Does this authority control or credibly summarize the question?

  2. Jurisdiction:

    Does it cover the right country, state, tribe, property, program, product, and person?

  3. Type:

    Is it law, rule, order, license record, recall, guidance, research review, form, or support service?

  4. Status:

    Is it current, effective, pending, proposed, archived, stayed, superseded, or alleged?

  5. Date:

    When was it effective, amended, reviewed, accessed, or scheduled to change?

  6. Object:

    Does it match the exact person, product, lot, license, model, job, trip, shipment, or claim?

  7. Evidence:

    Can another person open the same page, section, record, notice, or saved copy?

  8. Exit:

    What route handles care, a complaint, correction, appeal, professional question, or dated recheck?

Write “unknown” where the record is incomplete. Do not let a search snippet, generated summary, government seal, retailer paraphrase, social post, or cached screenshot silently fill a source gap.
