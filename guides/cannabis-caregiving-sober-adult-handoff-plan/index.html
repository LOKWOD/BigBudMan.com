Cannabis and caregiving safety plan

Care first.
Cannabis second.

When another person depends on you, the useful plan is not a guessed sobriety timer. It is a named sober adult, an explicit transfer of responsibility, locked storage, protected transport, and a response route if the plan fails.

Household planning for adults 21+—not medical advice, a child-custody opinion, a legal conclusion, a fitness-for-duty decision, an individualized impairment test, or permission to use cannabis while responsible for another person. Local law, care agreements, professional duties, medicines, pregnancy and breastfeeding, and the dependent person's needs can change the answer.

THE USEFUL PART

Keep these in your head.

  • A handoff is complete only when a named sober adult accepts the job, receives the information and access needed to do it, and confirms how long the responsibility lasts.
  • Do not turn elapsed time, route, tolerance, body size, or a home test into proof that someone is safe to supervise, drive, administer medicine, respond to choking or falls, or make an urgent decision.
  • Separate the caregiver's cannabis product from every medicine, food, drink, inhaler, topical, vape, and device used by the dependent person; keep cannabis locked, labeled, and in its original package.
  • Transport requires its own sober-driver and seat-transfer plan. A passenger handoff is not complete if the only available driver is impaired or cannabis is accessible in the vehicle.
  • Suspected child or dependent-adult exposure is a poison-response problem: call Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222 in the United States; call 911 for collapse, seizure, trouble breathing, or inability to wake.

A dependent person may need more than “an adult nearby.” Write the actual task: waking for feeds, lifting, supervising water, preparing measured medicine, responding to alarms, interpreting nonverbal distress, or driving to care. If the backup adult cannot safely perform that task, the handoff has not solved the problem.

Use closed-loop language: “I am handing care to you now. Do you accept responsibility until this named condition?” The receiving adult repeats the scope and confirms the plan. A text left without acknowledgement, a person who is asleep, or someone who has also used an impairing substance is not a completed transfer.

Build a two-layer access-control system

  1. Keep the retail package.

    Preserve the product name, responsible business, ingredients, cannabinoid information, warnings, lot, and response information. A child-resistant package is not childproof.

  2. Lock the complete cannabis set.

    Put flower, edibles, drinks, oils, topicals, vapes, batteries, chargers, tools, and waste in a locked enclosure that is out of sight and reach. Do not leave an active serving, cooling edible, or device outside the system.

  3. Separate look-alikes.

    Do not store cannabis candy, beverages, or baked goods beside ordinary food, medicines, supplements, or the dependent person's products.

  4. Control the key or code.

    Name the adult who holds access. Do not put a key beside the box, reuse a code a child knows, or disclose a code during a handoff unless the receiving adult needs it for emergency control.

  5. Re-lock immediately.

    The system fails during the open interval. Count products, close original packages, clear the surface, and verify the enclosure after every access.

Make transport a separate gate

  • Name the sober driver before cannabis use begins; do not decide after the trip becomes urgent.
  • Transfer the vehicle keys, child seat or mobility-equipment instructions, destination, contact information, and payment or access needs.
  • Keep cannabis closed and inaccessible in accordance with current state and local law; never leave a child, dependent adult, or animal alone in a vehicle.
  • Keep an alternate ride or emergency-services route available if the planned driver becomes unavailable.
  • Do not let symptom relief, confidence, a timer, or a short distance stand in for driving fitness.

CDC states that cannabis can slow reaction time and decision-making and impair coordination, memory, and judgment. The safest driving choice after cannabis use is not to drive and to arrange a sober driver.

Keep infant feeding and smoke exposure inside the plan

Pregnancy, breastfeeding, chestfeeding, infant care, and medicine questions need a clinician—not a household guess. CDC advises breastfeeding mothers to avoid marijuana or CBD because chemicals can pass through breast milk and data are insufficient to establish safety. CDC also says caregivers should not smoke marijuana around babies or children and notes that marijuana may impair caregiver judgment and the ability to care for an infant.

Do not smoke or vape in the home or vehicle around a child or dependent person. Changing rooms, opening a window, using fragrance, or running an air cleaner does not convert indoor smoking into a no-exposure event. Keep feeding supplies, bottles, medicines, and cannabis preparation areas separate.

Use an exposure response that does not depend on blame

  • CALL 911 NOWFor collapse, seizure, trouble breathing, severe confusion, or inability to wake.
  • CALL POISON CONTROLIn the United States, call 1-800-222-1222 for suspected cannabis exposure or use the webPOISONCONTROL tool. Do not wait for symptoms to appear.
  • DO NOT INDUCE VOMITINGFollow Poison Control or emergency instructions. Do not give a home remedy or force food or drink.
  • BRING THE EXACT PACKAGEKeep the product, package, ingredient list, cannabinoid amount, lot, missing quantity, time, and observed symptoms together.
  • PRESERVE THE SCENE SAFELYLock away remaining products, keep other people from exposure, and record facts without delaying care.
