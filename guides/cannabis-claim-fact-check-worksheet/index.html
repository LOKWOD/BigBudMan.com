Cannabis evidence and media literacy

Keep the claim.
Audit the evidence.

A citation can be real and still fail to support the product, person, outcome, timing, or certainty promised in the sentence beside it.

General consumer education for adults 21+—not medical, scientific, laboratory, or legal review. Do not delay diagnosis or treatment because of a product claim or this worksheet. Verify current rules and source records for the exact product, place, and date.

THE USEFUL PART

Keep these in your head.

  • Copy the full claim and surrounding image, headline, footnote, disclosure, product identity, URL, and date before evaluating it.
  • Name the claim type: identity, composition, safety, effect, disease, timing, legality, testing, certification, or comparison claims require different evidence.
  • Match the evidence to the exact product or formulation, route, amount, population, comparator, outcome, duration, and conditions; an ingredient study does not automatically validate a finished product.
  • Separate an official rule, a public-health recommendation, a scientific study, a laboratory result, a manufacturer instruction, and a personal story instead of ranking them by logo alone.
  • Record missing, conflicting, superseded, or inaccessible evidence as unresolved. A disclaimer, badge, influencer, AI summary, or large reference list cannot repair the gap.

Capture the net claim before it moves

Save the exact words, page or post URL, date, seller or speaker, product and variant, images, charts, footnotes, linked study, disclosures, and the action the message asks you to take. Screenshots help preserve a changing ad, but keep the live URL because a screenshot cannot show later corrections, link destinations, or page scope.

Do not isolate one careful sentence from an aggressive headline, product name, before-and-after image, white coat, badge, testimonial, or purchase button. The Federal Trade Commission evaluates both express and reasonably implied claims and the overall impression of an advertisement. The useful unit is therefore the complete message a reasonable reader could take away—not the marketer's preferred paraphrase after a challenge.

Name the kind of claim

Claim laneQuestion it answersEvidence that does not settle it alone
Identity or compositionWhat is in this exact package, in what form and amount?A product name, ingredient list without amounts, generic certificate, or another lot
SafetySafe for whom, at what exposure, with which medicines, route, duration, and monitoring?Natural, hemp, nonintoxicating, passed one panel, or no complaint shown
Effect or performanceWhat measurable outcome changed, compared with what, and for how long?Mechanism, animal or cell result, testimonial, aroma term, or satisfaction survey
Disease or treatmentDoes the exact intervention prevent, diagnose, treat, mitigate, or cure a condition?A structure/function phrase, clinician image, preliminary result, or unrelated approved drug
TimingWhen might an effect begin, peak, end, or leave residual impairment?A universal countdown, one person's experience, or a different route and formulation
Legal or regulatoryWhich rule controls this product, activity, person, place, and date?A federal hemp phrase, shipping offer, disclaimer, state map, or proposed bill
Testing or certificationWho tested or certified exactly what, to which method or standard, with what status?A seal image, QR code, laboratory logo, potency page, or self-created badge

One sentence can occupy several lanes. “Lab-tested hemp gummies clinically proven safe for sleep in every state” makes composition, testing, safety, treatment, population, and legal claims. Split them. One source is unlikely to answer all six.

Give every source a job and a boundary

Source recordUseful forBoundary to preserve
Statute, regulation, court record, or regulator pageThe rule, agency position, license, recall, warning, or required process in its jurisdictionNot clinical proof, universal law, or a substitute for checking effective dates and local rules
Systematic review or evidence assessmentA structured view of multiple studies for a defined questionCan inherit weak or mismatched studies; read population, products, outcomes, dates, and certainty
Controlled human studyA defined intervention, comparator, population, outcomes, duration, and harmsDoes not automatically transfer to another ingredient, dose, route, product, population, or outcome
Observational human studyAssociations, patterns, and uncommon or long-term questionsAssociation is not automatically causation; confounding and measurement limits matter
Animal, cell, or chemistry studyMechanisms, plausibility, hazards, methods, and future researchCannot by itself establish a consumer health benefit or clinical safety in people
Lot report or certificateMeasured results for the identified sample under the named method and reporting systemNot a human-outcome study, every-package guarantee, legal opinion, or proof of conditions after sampling
Manufacturer instructionsExact-model use, compatibility, warnings, maintenance, warranty, and intended conditionsCommercial source with limited scope; not independent comparative proof or a universal safety finding
Testimonial, rating, forum post, or personal logA question, signal, experience, or usability observation worth investigatingCannot establish general causation, prevalence, safety, authenticity, or a treatment effect

“Government source” is not one evidence level. A regulator can authoritatively explain the rule it administers while summarizing health evidence at a different level. A medical evidence review can interpret health research without deciding whether a local dispensary is licensed. Record the job each source is doing.

Run the nine-part evidence-transfer check

  1. Exact claim.

    Does the source test or establish the actual statement, including its certainty and implied meaning?

  2. Exact material.

    Is the evidence about the finished product, a named formulation, one purified compound, a different extract, or the plant broadly?

  3. Exact route and amount.

    Oral, inhaled, topical, transdermal, and prescription routes are not interchangeable. Keep stated amounts and units attached.

  4. Exact population.

    Age, diagnosis, medicines, pregnancy, prior exposure, setting, and exclusion criteria determine who was actually studied.

  5. Exact comparator.

    Placebo, usual care, another active product, baseline, no exposure, or a different group answer different questions.

  6. Exact outcome.

    A laboratory marker, rating scale, symptom report, function, diagnosis, adverse event, and driving task are not synonyms.

  7. Exact time.

    One exposure, several weeks, long-term use, and follow-up after stopping cannot be collapsed into “works.”

  8. Whole result.

    Read effect size, uncertainty, dropouts, adverse events, protocol, analysis, limitations, funding, corrections, and contrary findings—not only the abstract conclusion.

  9. Current status.

    Check whether the page, rule, label, study record, recall, or report was corrected, superseded, withdrawn, retracted, or replaced.

If any field differs, describe the transfer rather than hiding it: “This study tested a purified oral formulation in adults with condition X for Y weeks; it does not establish that this retail gummy has the same composition, exposure, benefit, or risk.” Missing is a result, not permission to generalize.

Print the page or copy these headings into notes. Preserve facts and boundaries rather than awarding a numerical “credibility score.” A single disqualifying mismatch—wrong product, withdrawn paper, unrelated outcome, or prohibited activity—should not be averaged away by several decorative strengths.

Treat these shortcuts as stop signs

  • “CLINICALLY PROVEN INGREDIENT”An ingredient study may not test the marketed product, amount, route, population, outcome, or claim.
  • “ONE STUDY SHOWS”Find the complete study, registration, corrections, limitations, effect size, and the rest of the relevant evidence.
  • “DOCTORS RECOMMEND”Ask which doctors, how selected, what question, which product, what evidence, and whether an endorsement is paid.
  • “FDA REGISTERED” OR AGENCY LOGORegistration, listing, a facility record, or a borrowed seal is not approval of every product or claim.
  • “LAB TESTED”Demand the exact lot, complete final report, laboratory identity, methods, units, panels, limits, qualifiers, and current status.
  • “NO SIDE EFFECTS”Absence from an ad, small study, review set, or personal experience is not proof of no risk.
  • “LEGAL IN ALL 50 STATES”Identify the exact product and activity, then check current federal, state, tribal, local, property, carrier, and program rules.
  • “MONEY-BACK GUARANTEE”A return promise is not evidence that a health, safety, quality, or legal claim is true.

Turn fact-checking into a better purchase exit

Before payment, keep the claim audit beside the ordinary product record. Verify the lawful seller, exact variant, package and lot identity, ingredients, serving and package math, complete current report, warnings, recall status, storage, return or complaint route, and final delivered cost. A claim that survives a science check can still belong to the wrong lot or an unlawful seller; a traceable package can still carry an unsupported treatment promise.
