Cannabis label field guide

Rebuild the product.
One field at a time.

The front panel is a pitch. The useful record connects the exact seller, sealed package, format, quantity basis, lot, dates, ingredients, warnings, storage instructions, and final lab report.

General label-reading education for adults 21+, using New York adult-use rules as a worked state example. It is not a nationwide compliance determination, dose recommendation, medical or legal advice, product authentication, or laboratory interpretation. The current regulator, exact package, final lot report, recall notice, and qualified professional control the decision.

THE USEFUL PART

Keep these in your head.

  • Identify the jurisdiction, licensed seller, responsible processor, exact format, package quantity, lot, and dates before interpreting a cannabinoid number.
  • Keep concentration, amount per serving, number of servings, total package amount, and net quantity in separate fields; they are not interchangeable.
  • A QR code is only a route. The destination must identify the same finished product and lot and expose the final report without inventing a match.
  • Record ingredients, allergens, warnings, storage, condition, recall status, and direct company contact before price or effect language influences the choice.
  • Write unknown when a field is absent or ambiguous. A blank is not zero, a marketing claim, or permission to borrow data from another package.

Run the identity pass before the potency pass

Do not begin with the largest number. Start by deciding what object the number belongs to.

  1. Jurisdiction and seller.

    Identify the state or other program and verify the exact retailer, address, and sales channel through the responsible regulator. A map result, copied license image, delivery text, marketplace badge, or brand website is not the live license record.

  2. Responsible company.

    Record the processor or manufacturer actually named on the package, its license information where required, and a direct contact route. Keep a separate brand or distributor field instead of treating every company name as the maker.

  3. Exact product.

    Copy the full name, format, variant, flavor or cultivar as labeled, count, net quantity, and universal or regulatory symbol. Do not merge similarly named products.

  4. Unique lot.

    Copy every character of the lot or batch identifier, including leading zeros, spaces, dashes, and suffixes. Photograph the code in place.

  5. Time and condition.

    Record purchase, package, test, expiration or use-by dates that are actually present, plus the seal, closure, leakage, damage, odor, and storage condition before opening.

Unknown is a valid result

If the package does not establish a field, mark it unknown and ask the licensed seller, responsible firm, or regulator. Do not complete the record from a menu photo, a similar lot, an old certificate, a search snippet, or memory.

Number on the labelWhat it answersCommon mistake
Net quantityHow much product or how many units the package contains, on its stated basis.Reading package weight as cannabinoid content.
ConcentrationHow much of a named constituent exists relative to weight or volume, often shown as a percentage or mass per volume.Treating a percentage as a dose, quality grade, or effect forecast.
Amount per servingThe labeled cannabinoid amount attached to one defined serving.Assuming one piece, sip, dropper, or container always equals one serving.
Servings per packageThe multiplier connecting a serving to the package total.Ignoring a partial or approximate serving count and inventing a clean division.
Amount per packageThe labeled total for the entire package on the named cannabinoid and basis.Comparing it with a different format, unit, cannabinoid, or laboratory basis.

Keep milligrams, grams, milliliters, percentages, counts, and laboratory units exactly as printed until the basis is understood. Never convert a dry-weight percentage, an as-received result, milligrams per gram, and milligrams per package as though they were the same field.

Change the field map when the format changes

FormatNumbers to keep separateExtra identity or safety fields
Flower or pre-rollNet weight; THC/THCA, CBD/CBDA, or total values and their stated basis.Cultivar as labeled, plain or infused status, ingredients or additions, harvest/package dates, and inhalation warning.
Edible or beverageAmount per serving, serving size, servings per package, package total, and net quantity or volume.Unit or division marks, full ingredients, major allergens, nutrition panel when required, delayed-effects warning, and remainder storage.
Oil, tincture, capsule, or softgelAmount per serving, serving definition, package total, container volume or count, and concentration if stated.Carrier oil, other ingredients, measuring device or fixed unit, route, storage, and medicine-interaction questions.
VapeNet quantity plus cannabinoid concentration or amount on the stated basis.Complete ingredient disclosure, hardware identity when provided, connector/device compatibility, leakage or damage, and inhalation warning.

This cross-format table is an editorial map, not a nationwide list of legally required fields. Rules vary by jurisdiction and product category. Use the current regulator for what must appear, then use the record to notice what still remains unknown.

Build a date chain instead of choosing the newest number

Date fieldUseful questionHard limit
Harvest or manufactureWhen did the relevant production event occur?It does not reveal every storage condition or establish freshness.
PackageWhen was this product placed into this retail package?It is not necessarily the harvest, production, or test date.
Sample or testWhen was the represented laboratory sample received or analyzed?It only helps after product and lot identity match.
Report issuedWhen was this report or revision released?A newer document is not automatically the final or correct lot report.
Expiration, use-by, or best-byWhat unopened shelf-life boundary does the responsible firm state?It cannot repair damage, a broken seal, improper storage, or a recall.
Purchase and openWhen did the reader obtain and first open the exact package?These are household record dates, not manufacturer claims.

Preserve the label wording. “Best by,” “expiration,” “manufactured,” “packaged,” “sample received,” and “report issued” answer different questions. Do not rename them to make the timeline look complete.

Treat ingredients, warnings, and storage as decision fields

  • Ingredients: copy active and inactive ingredients in order, including carrier oils, flavors, sweeteners, colors, caffeine or other added substances, terpenes, and anything grouped vaguely.
  • Allergens: record the exact major-allergen statement and any cross-contact language; absence of a voluntary “free from” badge is not an ingredient analysis.
  • Warnings: preserve the complete statements for age, children and pets, pregnancy or nursing, delayed effects, inhalation, driving or machinery, and topical-only use that apply to the product.
  • Instructions: separate a labeled serving from directions for use. Neither becomes a personal recommendation from this guide.
  • Storage: record temperature, light, moisture, refrigeration, resealing, upright position, or disposal instructions exactly as stated.
  • Contact: keep the responsible firm's direct phone or email, retailer, receipt, and regulator complaint route with the package record.

New York's current Part 128 guidance is one concrete example: it addresses full ingredients, major allergens, warnings, expiration, a lot identifier, storage conditions, responsible-processor details, and a route to the CoA. Another state or product category can require a different set. Do not copy the New York list into a nationwide compliance verdict.

Match the finished lot before reading the result

  1. Open the complete destination.

    A QR code, short link, or portal is only transport. Avoid unnecessary personal information and preserve the final downloadable report or official portal record.

  2. Match the responsible parties.

    Processor or manufacturer, product name, format, sample matrix, testing laboratory, and jurisdiction should describe the same finished product.

  3. Match the lot exactly.

    Do not accept a brand-level certificate, nearby lot, raw material, unfinished input, or report whose suffix differs.

  4. Check status and dates.

    Identify final versus preliminary or amended status, sample and report dates, and whether a regulator or laboratory note changes the meaning.

  5. Keep units and basis attached.

    Record analyte, result, unit, dry or as-received basis when stated, reporting limit, qualifier, pass or fail language, and the program threshold separately.

  6. Check required panels and notices.

    Use the current regulator to identify required testing and recall notices. A visible “pass” badge is not a substitute for the complete report.

A certificate describes a tested sample under a stated method. It does not prove that every unit is identical, guarantee an effect, establish nationwide legality, or override damage, storage failure, a recall, or a label mismatch.

Preserve a label-to-lot evidence card

WHEREJurisdiction · regulator · verified retailer · address or channel · purchase date · receipt
WHOResponsible processor/manufacturer · license when required · direct contact · brand kept separate
WHATExact product · format · variant · count · net quantity · seal and package condition
NUMBERSConcentration · amount/serving · serving definition · servings/package · package total · every unit and basis
LOT + DATESComplete lot/batch · manufacture/harvest · package · test · report · expiration/use-by · opened
CONTENTSIngredients · allergens · other active substances · solvents/additions where listed · hardware identity
INSTRUCTIONSWarnings · route · serving language · storage · reseal · disposal · child-resistant operation
REPORTDestination URL · final status · matching product/lot · lab · sample matrix · dates · panels · units · qualifiers
CURRENT STATUSOfficial recall/advisory search · date checked · exact match fields · required action
UNRESOLVEDEvery absent, ambiguous, contradictory, or unreadable field; question and official contact route

Keep front, back, bottom, seal, lot, receipt, and report images together without exposing personal information. This card is designed to support a seller question, professional review, complaint, adverse-event report, or recall match; it is not a compliance score.

Translate the claim without inheriting it

Claim or badgeWhat to ask forWhat it cannot establish by itself
Lab testedFinished-product lot, final report, required panels, status, dates, units, and qualifiers.Safety, authenticity, clinical benefit, or a result for another lot.
Full spectrum / broad spectrum / isolateOperational definition, exact cannabinoid and other-compound results, ingredients, and current rule.A universal composition, entourage effect, zero THC, or drug-test outcome.
Indica / sativa / hybridProducer, batch chemistry, route, amount, and the reader's actual response record.A guaranteed mood, energy level, sleep result, or medical effect.
Live / fresh / solventlessProcess definition, inputs, ingredients, residual-solvent panel where applicable, and final lot evidence.Purity, safety, freshness, or superiority.
Organic / natural / cleanExact certification or rule, scope, ingredients, process, contaminant results, and responsible certifier.Non-toxic, pesticide-free, medically suitable, or environmentally preferable in every respect.
Fast acting / extended releaseProduct-specific mechanism, serving instructions, evidence, onset range, duration limits, and regulator status.An instant, predictable, or safe driving timeline.
Wellness, sleep, calm, pain, focusExact wording, product-specific human evidence, route, amount, population, outcome, conflicts, and approval status.Treatment, prevention, diagnosis, or an outcome for this reader.

New York's adult-use guidance prohibits health claims on packages; federal FDA rules separately govern drugs, foods, supplements, cosmetics, and therapeutic claims. The applicable answer depends on the exact product and jurisdiction. Use the claim fact-check worksheet when the label moves beyond identity and into an outcome promise.
