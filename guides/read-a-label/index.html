Buying guide

Read the label.
Ignore the theater.

The front sells an identity. The regulated panel tells you what you are actually holding.

THE USEFUL PART

Keep these in your head.

  • Separate amount per serving from amount in the whole package.
  • For flower, percentages describe concentration—not quality or the exact effect.
  • Check ingredients, warnings, dates, lot information, and test-result access.
  • A QR code is useful only when it resolves to trustworthy batch information.

Do not assume a gummy, chocolate bar, beverage, or package is one serving. Product designs vary. If a product is intended to be divided, look for clear physical markings and storage instructions for the remainder.

Flower percentages: useful, incomplete, noisy

Flower labels often show THC, total THC, CBD, and sometimes total cannabinoids. Those numbers help compare concentration, but they do not measure freshness, cure, aroma, smoothness, contamination, authenticity, or personal fit.

Laboratory testing is a measurement process, not divine revelation. Small numerical differences between products may not translate into a meaningful difference for the user.

Ingredients and hardware matter

Edibles and beverages can contain allergens, sweeteners, colorings, caffeine, or other active ingredients. Vape labels may identify extract type, added terpenes, hardware, and battery information. Topicals can include fragrances and ingredients that irritate skin.

“Natural” is marketing language, not a complete safety review. Read the whole list.

Certificate of analysis: match, then interpret

  • Open the link without entering unnecessary personal information.
  • Match product name, producer, lot or batch number, and date.
  • Confirm cannabinoid results roughly align with the package.
  • Look for contaminant panels required by the regulator.
  • Be skeptical of a generic report that cannot be tied to the item in hand.

A certificate of analysis is evidence about a tested sample. It is not a promise that every unit is perfectly identical.
