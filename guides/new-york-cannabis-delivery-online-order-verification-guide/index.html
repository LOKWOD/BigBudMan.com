New York cannabis delivery buying guide

Verify the store
before the order.

Delivery can be lawful in New York, but a polished menu, map pin, social account, text message, or payment request is not a license. Build one evidence chain from OCM's current listing to the website, order, handoff, receipt, and exact product.

Verify the exact seller on the New York Office of Cannabis Management website immediately before ordering.

THE USEFUL PART

Keep these in your head.

  • Start on OCM's current dispensary verification list. Follow the website linked by the regulator instead of trusting a search ad, copied logo, map result, direct message, or look-alike domain.
  • New York's consumer delivery FAQ says an adult-use delivery order is prepaid, requires age attestation when ordered, and requires another valid-ID check plus a signature at delivery. Cash is not accepted by the delivery employee.
  • A legitimate seller record does not automatically authenticate every page, payment message, product, or person using its name. Match the domain, contact route, order details, delivery record, and product package.
  • Preserve the invoice or receipt, package, lot, certificate link, payment record, texts, emails, and direct observations. Put urgent health or safety needs ahead of a fraud or product complaint.

Build the chain from the regulator—not the ad

  1. Open OCM's list yourself.

    Use the official dispensary location verification page, not a sponsored search result or link sent in a message.

  2. Find the exact legal business and address.

    Record the listed name, physical location or delivery-only status, and regulator-linked website.

  3. Follow the listed website.

    Compare the full domain character by character. A copied name, logo, menu, certificate, or storefront photo does not authenticate a different domain.

  4. Check the service area.

    OCM notes that temporary delivery-only entries do not allow on-site or preorder pickup; the licensee's own site should identify the areas it serves.

  5. Use a known contact route.

    If a payment request, substitute item, or delivery instruction arrives from another number or account, stop and confirm through the regulator-linked website or its published phone number.

Separate an unusual payment from a verified order

The Federal Trade Commission warns online shoppers about sellers that insist on gift cards, wire transfers, payment apps, or cryptocurrency because those methods can make recovery difficult. A regulated-market limitation can affect ordinary payment choices, but it does not make an unexpected personal account, gift-card code, crypto wallet, pressure deadline, overpayment refund, or remote-access request credible.

  • Do not use a phone number, QR code, wallet address, or payment link supplied only in an unsolicited text, email, social post, or direct message.
  • Do not send a photo of the front and back of an ID through an improvised channel merely because a message uses a dispensary's name.
  • Do not install remote-access software, share a login code, or permit screen control to solve an order problem.
  • Do not pay a second person to “release,” “insure,” “legalize,” or “clear” a cannabis delivery.
  • When the channel changes, independently reopen the OCM listing and contact the business through its published route.

Use the ten-gate delivery screen

  1. OCM listing:

    the exact seller is currently listed.

  2. Domain:

    the order site is reached from the regulator record and matches exactly.

  3. Service area:

    the stated destination is served and the handoff location is permitted.

  4. Identity:

    the legal business name, site, phone, receipt, and delivery communication agree.

  5. Cart:

    products, quantities, substitutions, taxes or fees, and total are reviewable before payment.

  6. Payment:

    the payee and channel belong to the verified business; no pressure-only or hard-to-recover demand appears.

  7. Age gate:

    21+ attestation, valid-ID check, and signature expectations are clear.

  8. Package:

    the delivered item is sealed, labeled, traceable, and matches the invoice.

  9. Lot evidence:

    the exact product and lot connect to the certificate and current recall check.

  10. Exit route:

    return, missing-order, product complaint, payment dispute, and regulator routes are known.

Route the problem without destroying the evidence

For a medical emergency, dangerous behavior, fire, crash, or active threat, call 911. For an unexpected exposure or poisoning question in the United States, contact Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222. Do not delay care to finish an order audit.

For a missing or wrong order, contact the verified seller through its published route. For a product, licensee, or delivery concern, preserve the order and package record and use the responsible OCM route. For suspected payment fraud, contact the payment provider promptly and report through the appropriate consumer-protection or law-enforcement channel. The product complaint guide keeps health events, product defects, seller concerns, and fraud evidence in separate lanes.
