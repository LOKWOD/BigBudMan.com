Responsible use

Use it like
an adult.

Legal does not mean consequence-free. A few firm rules prevent most of the predictable mess.

9 min read Reviewed August 17, 2026Adults 21+

ON THIS PAGE

THE USEFUL PART

Keep these in your head.

  • Do not drive, boat, operate machinery, or supervise risky activity while impaired.
  • Do not mix impatience with delayed-onset products.
  • Keep cannabis locked, labeled, and separated from ordinary food.
  • Notice when use starts taking more than it gives.

Before consuming

  • Confirm everyone participating is 21 or older and freely choosing.
  • Verify the source, read the current package, and identify the format.
  • Arrange transportation and remove keys before anyone is impaired.
  • Check whether medications, health conditions, pregnancy, breastfeeding, or prior adverse reactions call for professional advice or abstaining.
  • Choose a place where smoking or vaping is permitted and will not expose others.

While using

Keep the package nearby. That sounds obvious, yet labels disappear precisely when someone needs the serving amount, ingredients, or product name. Do not turn a fast-onset product into a competition and do not turn a delayed product into a race.

!

No open cannabis in vehicles

In New York, neither drivers nor passengers may consume cannabis in a vehicle, moving or parked. Impaired driving is illegal.

Alcohol can make impairment less predictable. Adding more products makes it harder to know what caused an unwanted effect and when it may fade.

Afterward: close, count, lock

Return the product to its original child-resistant package. Count loose items when practical, place the package inside an odor-resistant container, and lock the container somewhere children, teenagers, visitors, and pets cannot access.

“Up high” is not the same as locked. A kitchen cabinet is not secure simply because an adult has to reach for it. Edibles deserve special attention because they can look like ordinary candy, baked goods, or drinks.

When someone feels too high

Stop consuming. Move to a calm, familiar place, reduce noise and stimulation, and stay with the person. Reassure them without arguing about how they feel. Do not drive them yourself if you are impaired.

Call 911 for chest pain, trouble breathing, seizure, loss of consciousness, severe confusion, dangerous behavior, or any situation that feels like an emergency. For accidental exposure or non-emergency poisoning guidance in the United States, call Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222.

When a habit needs an honest look

Frequency alone does not tell the whole story. Look for loss of control, cravings, using more than intended, failed attempts to cut back, conflict at home, slipping work, unsafe use, or continuing despite clear problems.

The useful question is not “Can I function?” It is “Is this choice improving or shrinking my life?”

A health professional or substance-use counselor can help without requiring a dramatic label. Taking a planned break is also a practical way to see what has become automatic.

A five-line household policy

01 · AGENo access or use by anyone under 21.
02 · STORAGEOriginal package, closed inner container, locked outer location.
03 · VEHICLESNo consumption and no impaired driving—ever.
04 · GUESTSOffer information, never pressure.
05 · EMERGENCYPoison Control 1-800-222-1222; 911 when urgent.

Big Bud Man standard: know the source, read the batch, protect kids and pets, and never drive impaired.
