Santiam hemp:
the record before the claim.

Two federal records can identify the filed cultivar and preserve the applicant's description. They cannot authenticate a current seed lot, predict a crop, supply a cannabinoid result, or make a finished product lawful everywhere.

THE USEFUL PART

Keep these in your head.

  • USDA's germplasm database identifies Santiam as Cannabis sativa L. cultivar PI 699469, received as seed in December 2021, tied to PVP application 202100173, and currently unavailable with no accession images.
  • The PVP applicant, Arcadia Biosciences, says Santiam came from proprietary inbred lines SR.1 and S1.19, with F1 seed produced in 2019 and trials in Oregon and California in 2020. That is an applicant account—not independent pedigree verification.
  • The application describes a full-season, upright hemp variety with dense terminal flowers, pink pistils, and flowering initiated at roughly 15 to 14.75 hours of light. Those statements define the filed comparison record; they do not promise performance in every seed lot or environment.
  • The PVP record shows an application filed January 5, 2021; the linked germplasm record lists the application as pending. A pending variety-protection record is not a product approval, chemistry result, or legal authorization.
  • Start every retail or production decision from the exact material in hand: responsible business, seed or clone lot, production record, package, matched laboratory report, recalls, and the current rules for production, sale, possession, shipping, and travel.

Read the applicant description at its actual size

The PVP application describes Santiam as a full-season, upright hemp cultivar with large, dense terminal flowers, pink pistils, and flowering initiated as day length falls to roughly 15 to 14.75 hours. It also supplies plant, leaf, petiole, flower, and seed measurements under the applicant's stated conditions. These details can help distinguish the filed variety description from another record.

They do not guarantee how an unknown seed lot will express in a different latitude, planting date, environment, field, container, stress condition, production system, or harvest. The application's disease and insect fields are marked not tested. Silence is not resistance, and an untested box should never be converted into a sales claim.

PVP

Protection is not product verification

A Plant Variety Protection application documents an applicant's ownership claim and filed description. It is not a seed certification, germination report, field inspection, consumer laboratory report, FDA approval, dispensary license, or guarantee that a current product meets a jurisdiction's hemp rule.

Use the BaOx comparison without borrowing BaOx

The application uses BaOx as its comparison variety and records differences in branching, color, flower traits, and measurements. That comparison is narrow: it helps the examiner understand why the applicant says Santiam is distinct. It does not make Santiam a BaOx selection, descendant, substitute, or equivalent retail product.

Keep the two public records separate. The site's BaOx field note explains BaOx's own applicant and accession evidence. Shared hemp context, a comparison table, pink pistils, dense flowers, or a similar market description cannot transfer one variety's pedigree, chemistry, photos, agronomy, or reputation to another.
