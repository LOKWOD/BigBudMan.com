BaOx:
the spelling matters.

The strongest record is not a copied strain story. It is a chain from an older field-trial name to a current USDA accession, with every chemistry, purpose, seed, and finished-lot claim kept in its own lane.

THE USEFUL PART

Keep these in your head.

  • USDA ARS GRIN lists G 33294 as Cannabis sativa L. cultivar BaOx, received as seed in 2022 from Cornell donors. The accession record says the top name was corrected after donor follow-up and preserves the earlier collector identifier “BoAx (a.k.a. BaOX).”
  • Older university and extension records use BaOx or Boax in CBD-floral research. That historical research context does not give every seed lot or retail package a permanent CBD, THC, terpene, aroma, effect, or compliance result.
  • USDA's current accession page records partial-inbred reproductive uniformity and includes curator remarks that the crop-use class was unclear. Preserve that uncertainty rather than silently rewriting the accession as one fixed commercial type.
  • A field-trial measurement belongs to the named seed source, plants, site, year, sampling point, method, and units. It is evidence about that trial—not a certificate for another harvest.
  • For a finished product, verify the responsible business, exact product, package, lot, dates, ingredients, laboratory report, recall status, and current jurisdictional rule. The cultivar name starts the question; it cannot finish it.

Spelling is evidence, not decoration. Save the title, institution, year, entry name, and seed-source field together. A search engine may merge BaOx, BaOX, BoAx, and Boax; an identity record should not.

Read the USDA accession as a repository record

The current GRIN page identifies G 33294 as cultivar BaOx, donated in the United States, maintained by the Northeast Regional Plant Introduction Station, received as seed on March 31, 2022, and supplied by Lawrence Smart and George Stack at Cornell University. It records the improvement status as cultivar and reproductive uniformity as partial inbred.

The same page preserves a donor-follow-up note about correcting the top name and a curator remark that crop use was unclear, with a possible fiber phenotype noted from observation. That is not a reason to erase older CBD-floral research. It is a reason to label the source and date of each statement and avoid turning “cultivar” into one permanent use category.

≠

Repository identity is not retail authentication

An accession can document material held by a genebank. It does not prove that a seller's seed, a plant photograph, a flower package, or an extract is that accession.

Use chemistry as measured evidence, not cultivar folklore

NC State Extension uses research data, including a 2019 clonally propagated BaOx set, to explain that CBD and THC production in floral hemp are related but biologically variable. Its worked equations belong to the reported data and assumptions. They are not a promise that another crop will stop at a chosen threshold or repeat one ratio.

The useful consumer conclusion is narrower: a CBD-forward name does not guarantee compliance, a specific percentage, or the absence of THC. Production programs use their current sampling and testing rules; finished products require their own exact-lot evidence and applicable limits.

Run the field-result transfer test

  1. Identity.

    Was the trial entry seed, clone, accession, breeder lot, or seller lot? Record the complete printed name and source.

  2. Site-year.

    Record location, season, planting date, soil or system, weather context, management, and harvest point.

  3. Design.

    Look for replication, randomization, sample count, comparison group, exclusions, and statistical method.

  4. Measurement.

    Keep plant part, sample timing, preparation, analyte, method, units, acid/neutral basis, and reporting limit attached to the result.

  5. Purpose.

    Separate breeding, agronomy, compliance, chemistry, disease, fiber, grain, floral biomass, and finished-product questions.

  6. Transfer.

    State what differs in the next field or package. If the identity or conditions change, the old number becomes context—not a forecast.

If the seller cannot connect its material to a credible identity record, the cultivar name remains an unverified sales claim. Do not repair the missing chain with an image match.

A finished package starts a new evidence chain

  • The responsible business and lawful seller can be verified through the current official route.
  • The exact product, form, net quantity, ingredients, allergen information when relevant, and warnings match the package.
  • The package lot matches the complete laboratory report character for character.
  • Sample matrix, dates, methods, units, reporting limits, qualifiers, panels, and report status are readable.
  • The product and lot are checked against current recalls and regulator notices.
  • Storage, seal condition, expiration or best-by language, and opened-on record remain attached.
  • No aroma, effect, treatment, safety, “THC-free,” or legality claim is inferred from BaOx alone.
