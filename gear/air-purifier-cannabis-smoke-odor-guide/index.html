Indoor-air gear guide

Filter the room.
Do not excuse the smoke.

Particles and gases are different jobs. Buy enough clean-air delivery for the room, demand real carbon for odors, and keep source control ahead of every machine.

12 min read Reviewed September 9, 2026Adults 21+

ON THIS PAGE

Independent indoor-air buying guidance for adults 21+. No purifier was purchased, tested, scored, or endorsed. Filtration cannot make indoor cannabis smoking safe, prevent impairment, erase exposure, or override smoke-free, lease, workplace, or property rules.

THE USEFUL PART

Keep these in your head.

  • The strongest control is not generating cannabis smoke indoors; ventilation with clean outdoor air and filtration are supplements, not permission.
  • Use smoke CADR and the actual room volume for particle sizing; a HEPA badge without airflow does not describe room-cleaning speed.
  • Odor and gases require substantial activated-carbon or other specified sorbent media; particle CADR does not rate gas removal.
  • Skip intentional ozone generation, vague square-foot claims, tiny decorative carbon sheets, unavailable replacement filters, and performance promises without a test method.

Size for smoke CADR, not an optimistic floor area

EPA recommends choosing a portable air cleaner with a clean air delivery rate, or CADR, large enough for the room. For small particles, the smoke CADR is the relevant published CADR—not pollen or dust. EPA's consumer guide estimates minimum smoke CADR near two-thirds of room area in square feet for an eight-foot ceiling: about 130 cfm for 200 square feet, 195 cfm for 300, and 260 cfm for 400.

Treat that table as a starting estimate, not a universal guarantee. Higher ceilings, open doors, connected rooms, leakage, placement, lower fan speeds, furniture, and ongoing pollution can reduce real-world performance. A maker's square-foot claim may use one air change per hour while another uses five; compare the underlying airflow and test condition.

÷

Room volume changes the job

Record length × width × ceiling height, then confirm the published smoke CADR and the fan speed at which it was measured. An open-plan floor is not several sealed rooms added together.

HEPA handles particles; carbon addresses some gases

A HEPA filter can be useful for fine airborne particles when the assembled machine moves enough air through it. “HEPA-type,” “HEPA-style,” and a filter-efficiency percentage do not replace a whole-unit smoke CADR. Also check whether the unit bypasses air around the filter and whether replacement media is available.

Odor involves gases as well as particles. EPA says activated carbon can help when there is a large amount of material, while noting that there is no widely used portable-air-cleaner rating system comparable to particle CADR for gas removal. Ask for carbon mass or bed depth, target pollutants, airflow, and a documented replacement cue. A thin black prefilter may collect lint yet have little gas capacity.

  • Demand the exact replacement filter part number and current availability before buying the machine.
  • Compare annual filter cost at the maker's stated schedule, but expect smoke and heavy odor loading to shorten useful life.
  • Reject washable-HEPA claims unless the manufacturer explicitly documents the filter as washable; water can damage ordinary high-efficiency media.
  • Do not vacuum, bake, sun, perfume, or chemically spray a saturated carbon filter unless the manufacturer specifically permits it.

The ozone gate is simple

Do not use an ozone generator in an occupied home. EPA describes ozone as a lung irritant and says that, at concentrations that do not exceed public-health standards, ozone is generally ineffective at controlling indoor air pollution. Ionizers, plasma, electrostatic, ultraviolet, and photocatalytic features require their own evidence and may not improve the two jobs you actually need.

Prefer a machine that can operate its particle and carbon filtration with optional ionizing or ozone-related features off. Check the manual, certifications, and state rules. California's Air Resources Board maintains a list and regulation for air-cleaning devices sold in California, but listing is not a cannabis-smoke performance endorsement.

The unglamorous purchase checks

  1. Noise at the useful speed.

    Compare sound data at the fan setting that produces the claimed CADR. A silent sleep mode that barely moves air cannot substitute for full-speed performance.

  2. Controls and privacy.

    Manual controls should work without an account, app, location permission, or cloud service. Confirm whether settings resume after a power loss.

  3. Electrical safety.

    Look for a recognized safety listing appropriate to the complete appliance, intact cords, stable construction, clearance instructions, and a filter interlock if specified.

  4. Filter access.

    A user should be able to identify, replace, and seal the correct filter without touching heavily loaded media unnecessarily.

  5. Ownership cost.

    Calculate replacement media and electricity at the speed and schedule you expect to use. Do not treat a low machine price with proprietary filters as low total cost.

  6. Return and warranty terms.

    Read noise, opened-filter, shipping, restocking, motor, sensor, and discontinued-filter terms before purchase.

Placement and operation matter

  • Use the purifier in the occupied room it is sized for, on a stable surface with every intake and outlet clearance the manual requires.
  • Do not hide it behind a sofa, curtain, cabinet, plant, wet area, heat source, or open flame.
  • Close uncontrolled entry paths when appropriate, but never block required ventilation, combustion-appliance air, a fire door, alarm, sprinkler, or emergency exit.
  • Run a higher speed when rapid particle reduction is needed and continue long enough after the source has stopped; filtration falls when airflow falls.
  • Replace filters by condition and maker guidance. Wear appropriate protection and bag dirty filters if the instructions call for it.
  • Keep the appliance, cord, removed filters, and all cannabis products away from children and pets.

If smoke enters from a neighbor or shared system, document the pattern and use the lease, building policy, management, housing, or health route described in the indoor-air guide. Do not alter common HVAC or building penetrations yourself.

A fast claim audit

  • “REMOVES 99.9%”Of what pollutant, in what chamber, on one pass or over time, at which speed, and measured where?
  • “COVERS 1,500 SQUARE FEET”At how many air changes per hour and with what ceiling height, doors, and test standard?
  • “ELIMINATES CANNABIS ODOR”What gas-phase method, carbon mass, airflow, loading condition, test duration, and replacement point support that claim?
  • “MEDICAL GRADE”Which recognized standard or regulatory status defines the phrase for this complete consumer unit?
  • “ZERO OZONE”Which operating modes were tested, by whom, under which limit, and can every optional feature stay off?
  • “REAL-TIME AIR QUALITY”Which pollutants does the sensor measure, over what range and tolerance? A color ring is not a cannabis-exposure test.

The honest buying shortlist

  1. Start with the rule:

    no indoor smoke around children, pets, pregnant people, guests, workers, or anyone who does not consent.

  2. Measure the room:

    area, ceiling height, doors, and connected spaces.

  3. Match particle performance:

    published smoke CADR at a tolerable operating speed.

  4. Match gas media:

    documented activated-carbon or sorbent quantity, target, and replacement path.

  5. Reject ozone:

    no intentional ozone generation in occupied space and no mandatory mystery mode.

  6. Price the ownership:

    machine, replacement filters, electricity, noise, space, warranty, and returns.

  7. Keep the limit visible:

    an air cleaner supplements source control and clean-air ventilation; it does not certify safety or remove impairment.
