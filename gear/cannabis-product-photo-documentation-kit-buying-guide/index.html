Product photo documentation kit buying guide

Photograph the record.
Not the mood.

A useful package photo is stable, legible, complete, dated, and connected to the original file. Build the smallest setup that can capture the whole object, every side, exact lot, seal condition, and close detail without glare or cosmetic editing.

THE USEFUL PART

Keep these in your head.

  • Start with the receiving route. FDA MedWatch, a state regulator, seller, manufacturer, recall process, payment dispute, clinician, or lawyer may ask for different images and data; a beautiful photo can still omit the necessary side or identifier.
  • Use the phone and daylight before buying gear. Purchase only when stability, repeatable angle, glare, small text, color, working distance, or hands-free capture remains an actual barrier.
  • A stand must fit the exact phone, case, camera position, controls, connector, object size, angle, and work surface. A ring light can create a bright reflection that erases the very lot code being documented.
  • Capture context first, then detail: unopened package when possible, all sides, seals, lot and dates, product condition, receipt or order record, and only then the close-up. Never discard or overwrite the original files.
  • Do not post labels, receipts, medical records, addresses, barcodes, account numbers, specimen identifiers, or government ID publicly. Share the minimum needed through the verified official route.

Run the no-purchase setup first

  1. Clean the lens.

    Use the phone maker's safe cleaning method. A smear can mimic haze or erase small text.

  2. Use a stable surface.

    Rest forearms, prop the phone securely without blocking ventilation, or ask a trusted person to hold it. Do not improvise a top-heavy rig over breakable items.

  3. Find diffuse light.

    Move near indirect daylight or bounce an existing lamp off a pale wall. Turn off mixed-color lights when they distort label color.

  4. Move the object, not digital zoom.

    Fill the frame while keeping the entire relevant edge and context. Use the phone's supported close-focus mode only when it remains sharp.

  5. Control reflections.

    Rotate the package or light slightly. Do not remove, flatten, cut, or peel a seal merely to defeat glare.

  6. Capture a sequence.

    Start wide, work around the object in order, then take detail views. Review legibility before changing the scene.

Color accuracy can help document a visible condition, but a consumer photo does not identify mold, a contaminant, chemical composition, or authenticity. Do not call a light or color card “forensic” unless the exact system and workflow carry a legitimate documented standard.

Use references without making a measurement claim

  • Place a clean ruler or scale card in the same plane as the feature, without covering the object.
  • Keep the reference parallel to the relevant surface and photograph the whole reference plus the detail.
  • Record the reference's printed units; do not convert pixels into an exact dimension from an angled image.
  • Use a neutral, matte, uncluttered backdrop that contrasts with the package without suggesting a laboratory setting.
  • Include a handwritten case note or image number beside—not on—the package when the receiving route permits it.
  • Do not add arrows, circles, color correction, filters, background removal, object erasure, or generative fill to the original.

Create a separate annotated copy only when useful, and retain the untouched original plus an edit note. A ruler shows approximate visible scale; it does not calibrate the camera or certify a dimension.

Use a twelve-frame capture sequence

  1. Scene and storage context.
  2. Front of the unopened or current package.
  3. Back panel.
  4. Left and right sides.
  5. Top and bottom.
  6. Closure, seal, or tamper-evident feature.
  7. Exact product name, form, count, and net quantity.
  8. Lot or batch, manufacture/package date, expiration or best-by date.
  9. Responsible business, license or contact information, and required warnings.
  10. Ingredients, allergens, serving or unit language, and directions when relevant.
  11. Affected area with context and a same-plane scale reference when safe.
  12. Receipt, shipping label, menu/listing, report, or recall record captured separately with private data minimized.

Do not open a package that should remain sealed for a return, regulator, laboratory, insurer, or legal review. Ask the receiving organization before changing the condition.
