THE USEFUL PART

Keep these in your head.

  • Compare exact models on actual input watts, complete PPFD-map context, controls, mounting, environmental limits, warranty, parts, and seller support—not equivalent-watts slogans.
  • The three-option calculator combines fixture price, count, required setup, annual maintenance, and light-only electricity over one shared horizon; unknown inputs must stay visibly unknown.
  • A light-only estimate excludes fans, pumps, humidification, dehumidification, heating, cooling, controls, taxes, demand charges, crop loss, labor, and unplanned failure.
  • No calculator approves a branch circuit or predicts yield, potency, plant health, uniformity, compliance, or value. Preserve the evidence used for every comparison.

Clear the law and room before comparing products

Home-cultivation rules vary by state, locality, residence, program, age, plant maturity, access, visibility, lease, and property. New York's current OCM home-cultivation overview is one official example: it addresses adult plant limits, secure possession, odor mitigation, electrical load, fire risk, moisture, and mold. It is not a national rule. Start with the 50-state library and the agency that regulates the exact address.

Measure the usable cultivation footprint, ceiling height, permitted hanging structure, door and work clearance, water path, ventilation, panel access, alarms, and exit route. Record ambient conditions and every existing electrical load. A fixture can physically fit while still violating required clearance, exceeding a mounting point, raising cooling or dehumidification demand, or sharing an unsuitable circuit.

Save the manual and complete map as files or screenshots tied to the model and date. A marketplace listing can change. PPF describes fixture output; PPFD describes density at a surface. A complete comparison keeps units, height, footprint, setting, and measurement context together instead of copying the highest visible number.

Enter the shared operating assumptions and complete exact-model costs. Blank options stay unknown; enter zero only when a documented cost is truly zero.

Annual energy = actual watts × fixture count × hours/day × days/year ÷ 1,000. Annual electricity = annual kWh × entered rate. Acquisition = fixture price × count + required setup. Horizon total = acquisition + (annual electricity + annual maintenance) × years. Taxes, financing, fans, pumps, controls, humidification, dehumidification, heating, cooling, installation labor, demand charges, crop loss, downtime, and unplanned failure are excluded unless captured in a stated input.

Math check: two 300-watt fixtures at $250 each, $100 required setup, zero annual maintenance, 12 hours daily, 365 days yearly, $0.20/kWh, and three years equal a 600-watt light load, 2,628 kWh and $525.60 light electricity per year, $600 acquisition, $2,176.80 horizon total, and $725.60 annualized.

Keep light cost separate from room load and circuit design

The calculator covers the entered fixtures only. Build a second inventory for exhaust and circulation fans, pumps, controllers, sensors, humidifier, dehumidifier, heater, air conditioner, cameras, chargers, and anything else connected to the room. Some loads cycle, start differently, or vary with weather. A nameplate inventory is useful evidence for a qualified electrician; it is not a do-it-yourself circuit verdict.

  • Record the panel and circuit serving the room without removing covers or touching wiring.
  • List every connected device, exact model, documented voltage, input, plug, cord, and expected schedule.
  • Keep water, irrigation, condensation, and wet work away from electrical equipment as the instructions require.
  • Do not daisy-chain power strips, use improvised adapters, defeat grounding, bury cords, or suspend fixtures from unsupported hardware.
  • Stop using equipment with damaged cords, loose plugs, scorch marks, unusual heat, arcing, repeated trips, water exposure, or a recall.

New York OCM specifically warns that indoor grows can use significant electricity, creating fire risk and overload, and recommends LED lighting and consulting an electrician to confirm the home can support the load. That is a safety prompt, not approval of any fixture or setup.

The lowest calculated cost is not automatically the best purchase. The worksheet cannot score coverage quality, fit, noise, service, repairability, environmental burden, safety, or lawful use. Preserve the assumptions and evidence date so a future comparison can show what changed.

Run the checkout and after-sale check

  1. Match the model everywhere.

    Listing, cart, manual, map, warranty, plug, controls, and delivered label should describe the same exact fixture and revision.

  2. Price required setup.

    Include documented hangers, controller, timer, cables, mounting, safe support, electrician review, and other necessities—not optional product bundles presented as essential.

  3. Save the exit terms.

    Capture return deadline, opened-item and installation exclusions, restocking fees, shipping responsibility, warranty claimant, serial requirement, support route, and recall search.

  4. Inspect before energizing.

    Check for shipping damage, mismatched identity, missing parts, damaged cords or plugs, moisture exposure, and instructions that conflict with the intended room.

  5. Keep records.

    Save receipt, seller, date, serial, manual, warranty, support conversation, inspection notes, and any professional installation record.
